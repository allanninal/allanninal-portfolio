What a commission buys

Usually a first performance, not the music.

The most misunderstood transaction in the field. A commissioner who believes they bought the music has usually bought a first performance, and neither side finds out until somebody wants to record it.

Commission fee
For the writing
It pays for the piece to exist and for you to give the premiere. It does not, on its own, transfer anything.
Exclusivity period
12 months
Nobody else may perform it during that window. This is what a commissioner is usually actually buying and it is worth having in writing.
Copyright
Stays with me
Unless we agree otherwise in writing and the fee reflects it. This is the default in concert music and the opposite of the default in film.
Performance rights
Through the society
Licensed collectively rather than by me. It is why a second performance costs a programmer nothing extra to arrange.
Recording
Separate consent
A commercial recording is its own conversation. Free to agree, and not automatically included.
Buyout
Possible, priced
If you genuinely need to own it outright, say so at the start. It roughly doubles the fee and I will tell you honestly when you do not need it.

Briefs

5 adjectives, translated.

  1. "Epic but intimate" Usually: loud in one place and quiet everywhere else, with a solo line somebody can follow. Send instead Name the moment you want people to feel it, and how long the piece has to be.
  2. "Not too modern" Almost always: with a tonal centre. It is rarely about dissonance and usually about whether the ear knows where home is. Send instead Send two pieces you have programmed that audiences liked, and one you regretted.
  3. "Something like the temp" That the temp track already solved the problem, which is useful information rather than an insult. Send instead Say which twenty seconds of the temp, and what it is doing there — tempo, register, or the fact that nothing happens.
  4. "Accessible" Playable by this ensemble in the rehearsal time they actually have. Send instead Tell me the number of rehearsals. It changes more about the piece than any adjective can.
  5. "Make it more emotional" That a transition is not earning its arrival. Send instead Point at the bar. Nine times in ten the problem is thirty seconds earlier than the place it is felt.

None of this is a complaint about people who commission music. Adjectives are how anybody talks about sound before there is a score to point at — the useful move is to translate them quickly and in writing, rather than to write eleven minutes of the wrong piece politely.

Common questions

Mostly about the three that were never played.

Why publish performance counts?

Because it answers the question a programmer is actually asking — has anybody taken this risk before me — and because the alternative is a list that implies every piece is equally alive. A small number of pieces travel and most do not. That is the field, not a confession.

Three works have never been played. Should I be worried?

Two of them were written for situations that fell through, which happens constantly and says nothing about the music. The third I withdrew myself and it stays listed because a catalogue that hides its beginning is not a catalogue.

Can I see a score?

Yes, any of them, free and without a form. Perusal scores exist to be read and a composer who makes you ask twice is costing themselves performances.

Do I own the music if I commission it?

You own the premiere and an exclusivity period, and the copyright stays with me unless we agree otherwise and the fee reflects it. That is the norm in concert music and the reverse of the norm in film, which is where most of the confusion comes from.

How long does a commission take?

For a ten to fifteen minute chamber piece, four to six months from a signed agreement, of which the writing is about six weeks. The rest is the piece being wrong and then becoming right.

Will you write for amateur or student players?

Gladly, and it is harder rather than easier. The constraint that matters is rehearsal count, not ability — tell me how many and I will write something that can actually be finished.
