Paint Depth Log.

A gauge reading on its own tells you nothing. It becomes useful when you know the band it falls in, what the panel beside it reads, and how many microns the pass you are about to make will spend. All three are on this sheet.

Reading bands

Clear coat depth bands, what each means and what to do
ReadingWhat it meansWhat to do
Under 25 µm At risk. Do not cut. Protect only. Coating or sealant, no machine correction.
25–35 µm Thin. Previously corrected, or a light factory coat. One finishing pass at most, and only if the defect warrants it.
36–55 µm Normal factory range. Full correction options available.
56–90 µm Heavy factory coat, or a blend edge. Treat as factory but re-measure across the panel.
Over 100 µm Refinished. Almost certainly repainted. Lighter pass — refinish clear is softer and cuts faster.

A factory panel at the bottom of the normal range (40 µm) has about 15 µm of usable clear above the 25 µm floor. Everything below is how that gets spent.

What each pass spends

Microns removed by each level of correction
PassTypical spendRange
Wash and decontaminate 0 µm Nothing is removed from the paint.
Finishing polish 2 µm Typical. Range 1–3.
One-step correction 4 µm Typical. Range 3–5.
Two-step correction 8 µm Typical. Range 6–10.
Wet sanding, spot 10 µm Typical. Range 5–15, measured panel only.

Taking the readings

  1. 1 Take three readings per panel: top, middle and bottom. Paint is not uniform.
  2. 2 Compare against the neighbouring panel, never against a table. The step between panels is what identifies a repaint.
  3. 3 Plastic bumpers read differently from steel. Do not compare them to metal panels.
  4. 4 Aluminium panels read differently again on some gauges. Know which your gauge compensates for.
  5. 5 Record the reading before any machine work, not after. Afterwards it is evidence of what you took.

Before you agree to anything

  • ☐ Ask what the panel measured, not what the car measured. There is no such thing as a car reading.
  • ☐ Ask how many microns the proposed pass is expected to remove.
  • ☐ Ask what the reading will be afterwards, and whether that leaves room for a next time.
  • ☐ If a shop does not own a gauge, it is estimating with something it cannot replace.
  • ☐ Get the survey in writing. It is the baseline for anything that happens later.
