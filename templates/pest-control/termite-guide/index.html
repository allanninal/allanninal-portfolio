Termite Guide

The one pest on this site where waiting is measured in thousands of dollars, and the one where the treatment choice has real trade-offs rather than a single right answer.

How they get in

Soil-to-wood contact
Any point where framing, siding or a deck post touches soil. The single most common entry we find.
Foundation cracks
They need a gap of about 1/32". Settlement cracks qualify.
Mud tubes over the block
When there is no crack, they build a covered road up the wall. Pencil-width, brittle, on the shaded side.
Plumbing and utility penetrations
Unsealed penetrations through a slab are a straight route in with no tube required.

What a real inspection covers

  • ✓ Full crawlspace or basement perimeter, with a light held at grade
  • ✓ Sounding of accessible sills, joists and band boards
  • ✓ Moisture reading anywhere the wood sounds soft
  • ✓ Exterior grade check — soil depth against siding, mulch depth, deck posts
  • ✓ Swarm evidence: discarded wings at sills and window tracks
  • ✓ A written report with photographs, whether or not anything is found

Disclosure, and what bites at closing

  1. North Carolina requires known material defects to be disclosed on the Residential Property Disclosure Statement — past termite damage and treatment included
  2. A wood-destroying insect report (the WDIR / “termite letter”) is routinely required by lenders, and is a snapshot rather than a warranty
  3. Prior treatment is not a defect; concealing it is
  4. Keep the treatment records and the transferable warranty — both add value at closing
  5. A clear inspection today says nothing about last year, which is why buyers ask for the history as well as the letter

General information, not legal advice. Rules change and closings differ — ask your attorney or agent.
