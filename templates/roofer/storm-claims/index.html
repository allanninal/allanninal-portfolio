What We Hand The Adjuster

A claim is won on documentation, not on argument.

Adjusters are not the enemy and they are not being difficult. They settle from what they can see and defend. The difference between an approval and a denial is almost always whether the damage was recorded properly, on the day, in a form that survives review. This is what we produce for every claim, before anyone climbs the roof.

Slope diagram

Non-member A roof sketch, sometimes
Plan member Every slope, measured and numbered

Aerial measurement of every facet with pitch and square footage, so the scope covers the whole roof rather than the two slopes someone walked.

Test squares

Non-member One, if asked
Plan member One per damaged slope, chalked

A ten-by-ten square marked and counted on each slope, the way a carrier expects to see it. Most denials we overturn are slopes nobody squared.

Photographs

Non-member A handful from the ground
Plan member Dated, close-range, per strike

Close photographs with a gauge in frame, plus wide shots that place each one on the roof. A photograph nobody can locate proves nothing.

Collateral evidence

Non-member Rarely recorded
Plan member Vents, gutters, screens, AC fins

Soft metal dents where hail hit. It is the strongest corroboration there is, because it dates the event and nobody can call it wear.

Storm verification

Non-member Left to the carrier
Plan member Date, hail size, wind speed

Weather data for the address on the date claimed. It closes off the argument that the damage came from some other, older event.

Code requirements

Non-member Often missed
Plan member Cited, with the ordinance

Ventilation, ice-and-water and decking rules that force a rebuild beyond a like-for-like patch. Payable when your policy carries the endorsement, and easy to leave on the table when nobody raises it.

In Writing

The promises worth making are the ones you can hold us to.

Roofing has a reputation, and it was earned by companies that arrive after a storm and leave before the warranty matters. These are the commitments we put in writing on every job, including the ones that cost us work.

We will never offer to waive your deductible

It is insurance fraud in Oklahoma, it can void your claim, and the money has to come out of the work somewhere you cannot see. Any roofer who offers it is telling you what they are willing to do to a roof.

We will tell you when you do not have a claim

Plenty of roofs we inspect after a storm are undamaged, or damaged in ways a carrier will not pay for. We say so and put it in writing. A free inspection that always finds a claim is a sales call.

Our invoice matches the approved scope

We bill to the scope the carrier approved, with decking priced per sheet up front. No line items appear on the final invoice that were not discussed before the work started.

We are still here in ten years

Sixteen years in the metro, twelve people on staff, one office you can drive to. A workmanship warranty is only worth the company standing behind it, which is the whole problem with the trucks that show up after a hailstorm.

Nothing here is unusual for a roofer who intends to keep working in the same city. It is written down because, after a big storm, plenty of the people knocking on your door do not.
