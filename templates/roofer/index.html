Materials

What goes on the roof, and what it costs you later.

The material decides the price, the lifespan and how your insurer treats it, all at once. Here is the honest version of each, including where it is the wrong choice.

  • Architectural asphalt shingle

    Expected life
    25 to 30 years
    Best for
    Most homes in the metro

    The default for good reason: impact-resistant Class 4 options can lower your premium, and repairs are cheap because every crew stocks the parts.

  • Standing-seam metal

    Expected life
    40 to 70 years
    Best for
    Long-term owners, steep slopes

    Costs roughly double asphalt and pays back over decades. Sheds hail well, but a dented panel is cosmetic rather than structural, and insurers treat it that way.

  • Concrete or clay tile

    Expected life
    50+ years
    Best for
    Homes already framed for the weight

    Beautiful and long-lived, but heavy. If a house was not built for tile the framing has to be checked first, and that is an engineer’s call rather than a roofer’s.

  • TPO or EPDM membrane

    Expected life
    20 to 30 years
    Best for
    Flat and low-slope sections

    Most homes in the area have at least one low-slope section over a porch or addition. It cannot take shingles, and putting them there anyway is a leak with a date on it.

How It Goes

Five steps, and you know the number before step four.

  1. 1

    Free inspection

    We are on the roof within 48 hours, usually sooner after a storm. Photographs of everything we flag, and a written report whether or not you use us.

  2. 2

    The honest number

    A written scope with the material, the underlayment, the ventilation and the decking allowance spelled out. If it is a repair rather than a replacement, that is what you get quoted.

  3. 3

    Claim, if there is one

    We file with you, meet the adjuster on the roof, and hand over our own documentation. You pay your deductible; we do not offer to waive it, because that is insurance fraud.

    The work

    Most residential roofs are one or two days. Magnetic sweep of the whole property every evening, and again on the last day. Your yard should look untouched.

  5. 5

    Final walk and warranty

    We walk it with you, then issue the 10-year workmanship warranty and register the manufacturer warranty in your name. Both arrive by email the same week.

How do I know if I have hail damage? Nothing is leaking.
Most hail damage does not leak for a year or more. It bruises the mat under the granules, the granules wash off, and the shingle then ages several times faster. That is why claims get filed late and denied. If a storm dropped hail of any size on your street, get it looked at while the claim window is open.
How long do I have to file a hail claim?
In Oklahoma most policies give you one to two years from the date of the storm, but the practical limit is shorter: the older the damage, the harder it is to tie to a specific date, and insurers do deny on that. If you think you were hit, get it documented now even if you do not file.
Will you waive my deductible?
No, and you should walk away from anyone who offers to. It is insurance fraud in Oklahoma, it voids your claim, and it means the work is being funded by cutting something you cannot see from the ground.
Do I have to use the contractor my insurer recommends?
No. You choose the contractor. Your insurer decides what it will pay, which is a different question. A preferred-vendor list is a convenience, not a requirement.
Can you just go over the old shingles?
We do not. A lay-over hides the decking, which is exactly where the expensive surprises live, it voids most manufacturer warranties, and it adds weight to a structure that was designed for one layer. It is cheaper on the day and more expensive over the life of the roof.
How long does a replacement take?
A typical single-family roof is one to two days from tear-off to final sweep. Steep pitches, metal and tile take longer. We give you the number before we start, and we do not leave a roof open overnight.
What does the warranty actually cover?
Two separate things. The manufacturer warranty covers the material and is registered in your name. Our 10-year workmanship warranty covers installation: flashing, fastening, ventilation and anything we did. Nearly every early roof failure is workmanship, so that is the one that matters most.
Do you do gutters at the same time?
Yes, and it is the right time to do it. Gutters have to come off for a proper tear-off anyway, so replacing them during the roof costs less than doing it as its own visit.
