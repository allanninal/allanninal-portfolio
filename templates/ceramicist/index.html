A pottery site usually shows finished objects: glazed, cool, on a shelf, at one size. This one shows the arithmetic a gallery photo hides — the fired size, computed from the clay body's own shrinkage rate, and the loss rate, computed from every kiln load, not asserted in a paragraph. The two places on this page that discuss the same fact are mechanically the same fact.

What the label doesn't say

Cone & food-safety table

A vitrified body absorbs under 0.5% water and is dishwasher- and microwave-safe; an under-fired one is porous, regardless of what a listing says. Custom work

Commissions

A commission's price depends on size, form complexity and glaze, so it isn't quoted here — it's quoted after the conversation below. The lead time is not a scheduling inconvenience; it's the physics of the process, realistically 3–5 weeks end to end.

  1. Throw The piece is thrown to order, sized against the same shrinkage math the catalogue uses.
  2. Dry 3–7 days, slow and covered, so the walls don’t dry faster than the base and open an S-crack.
  3. Bisque fire A first, lower firing hardens the clay enough to handle and glaze.
  4. Glaze Glaze is applied to the bisqued piece by hand, dipped or brushed depending on the form.
  5. Glaze-fire Cone 6, oxidation, the same kiln and schedule as every firing in the log above.
  6. Cool A full, unhurried cool through the 573°C quartz inversion — the point most dunting cracks happen if it’s rushed.

FAQ

Questions

Why did my mug cost more than the one on Etsy?

That price already contains the third of a kiln load that didn’t survive the fire — the log above states the studio’s own rate, about 1 in 9. A price that doesn’t account for loss is a price that hasn’t looked at its own kiln.

Is this dishwasher safe?

If the body vitrified at cone 6 and the glaze is food-safe, yes — see the Cone & food-safety table below for the specific body/glaze pairs this studio uses, computed, not just claimed on a label.

Why do two mugs from the same firing look slightly different?

Kiln placement and the same shrinkage math: a mug nearer the element sees a slightly different heat-work curve than one on the cool side of the shelf, and even a stated 12.5% shrinkage rate is an average, not a guarantee down to the millimetre.
