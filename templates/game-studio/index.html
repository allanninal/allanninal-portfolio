Pay

The bands, on the public page.

"Competitive" is the least checkable word in the language. These are the real bands, they are the same for everyone at a level regardless of what somebody negotiated, and they are on this page rather than revealed at offer stage.

Salary bands, 2026
Level Band Note
Junior — any discipline $58k – $68k Zero to two years. We hire one or two a year and we do not expect them to arrive knowing our engine.
Mid — any discipline $78k – $95k The band most of the studio sits in. Movement within it is annual and written down.
Senior — any discipline $102k – $124k Discipline does not change the band. A senior artist and a senior programmer are paid the same here.
Lead / director $128k – $145k Four of the nine. Leads are still expected to do the craft, which is why the band is not wider.
Profit share 8% of net Split evenly by headcount, not by seniority. It paid out twice in seven years and both times it was small.

What we do not do: equity instead of salary, unpaid trials, or "we will revisit it after launch". If any of those turn up in a conversation with us, it is a mistake and you should say so.

The crunch record

3 periods in 7 years. 12 weeks. 1 unpaid.

Seven years, two shipped titles, and three periods where people worked more than their contracted hours. Here they are, with what caused each one and whether it was paid. A studio that claims zero is either very lucky, very new, or not counting.

  1. Oct – Nov 2021
    6 weeks     Fathom Line certification A platform submission failed twice on a memory bug nobody had budgeted for. Six weeks at roughly fifty hours. Paid at 1.5×, and everyone took the following two weeks off. Paid
  2. Mar 2023
    2 weeks     A festival build We chased a showcase deadline we did not need to hit. Two weeks, unpaid because we were between funding, and it is the one on this list we should simply not have done. Unpaid
  3. Aug – Sep 2024
    4 weeks     Kettle Row launch window A launch-week bug affecting saves. Four people, four weeks, paid, and we shipped the fix in six days. This is the kind we think is defensible and we would still rather not have had it. Paid

The policy behind it

  • — Contracted hours are 37.5. Core hours are 10 to 4, Monday to Thursday.
  • — Overtime is paid at 1.5× or taken back as time, the person’s choice, decided by them and not by a lead.
  • — Nobody schedules work in the last two weeks of December.
  • — If a date needs crunch to hit, the date moves first. That has happened twice and both times it cost us less than the crunch would have.

Publishing this is uncomfortable and it is the only version of a crunch policy worth having. "We do not crunch" is unverifiable; "we crunched three times in seven years, here is when, and one of them was our own fault" is something you can hold us to at an interview.

Before you apply

5 things we cannot offer you.

  • − A big-studio salary. Our senior band is real and it is below what a large publisher pays in a coastal city.
  • − Full remote. We are hybrid with three days in Lawrence, because a nine-person team loses something we have not worked out how to replace.
  • − A fast promotion track. There are four leads and nine people; the arithmetic is not in your favour and pretending otherwise wastes your time.
  • − Work on a huge franchise. Our games sell in the tens of thousands and that is the plan.
  • − Certainty past 2027. We are funded through Q3 and we will tell the team the moment that changes, as we have both previous times.

Common questions

Mostly about the bands.

Why publish salary bands?

Because "competitive" means nothing, because it stops the person who negotiates hardest being paid the most for the same work, and because it saves both sides a conversation that would otherwise happen in round three. It also means we cannot quietly underpay somebody, which is the actual reason most studios do not do it.

Do you really only crunch three times in seven years?

Three periods where people worked beyond contracted hours, yes, and they are listed with dates. If we have forgotten one, somebody who worked here will tell us and we will add it. That is the point of writing it down.

Why are there no team photos?

Because a headshot tells you nothing about who to email. The team list gives you a name, what they do and what to come to them for, which is what a visitor actually needs. This is also a website template, and the nine people are fictional — using stock portraits as fake employees would be inventing identities rather than inventing a company.

Are you hiring outside Kansas?

Not right now, and we say so plainly rather than letting people apply and find out. We are hybrid with three days in Lawrence. If that changes it will change on this page first.

Do you do unpaid test tasks?

No. Our task is four hours and it is paid at the mid contract rate whether or not you get the job. A studio that asks for unpaid work is telling you how it will treat you later.

What happens if the funding runs out?

We tell the team, immediately, as we did in 2022 and again in 2023. Both times we had six months of notice and both times nobody was surprised. We would rather lose people to a planned exit than to an announcement.
