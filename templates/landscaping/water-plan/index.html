Water Plan

In a high-desert valley the difference between a working irrigation schedule and the factory default is most of a summer water bill.

The schedule

Irrigation runs per week by month
PeriodRunsNote
AprilStart-up onlyCharge the system, walk every head, fix winter breaks. Cool nights mean the lawn does not need it yet.
May1–2 per weekDeep and infrequent from the first run. Short daily cycles now train shallow roots that fail in July.
June2 per weekAbout an inch a week total, split across two runs. Measure with a tuna tin, not a guess.
July – August2–3 per week, or noneEither commit to the inch, or let it go dormant and stop. Half-watering is the worst of both — it costs money and keeps the crown alive without greening the blade.
September2 per weekThe recovery month. This is when the water actually buys you something.
October1 per week, then stopTaper. Last run mid-month in most years.
NovemberBlow-outBefore the first hard freeze. Non-negotiable in this valley.

Setting the controller

  • ✓ Water between 4am and 8am — evening runs invite fungus, midday runs evaporate
  • ✓ Cycle and soak: three short runs ten minutes apart beat one long run on any slope or clay
  • ✓ Turn OFF the seasonal-adjust "smart" percentage until you know the baseline it is adjusting from
  • ✓ Rain sensor tested every spring — most failures are the sensor, not the controller
  • ✓ Write the programme on a label inside the controller door, because you will not remember it in March

Dormancy, honestly

Is dormancy the same as death?
No. Cool-season grass shuts down above roughly 85°F and resumes when it cools. The crown survives on very little; it is the blade that browns.
How little water keeps it alive?
Roughly half an inch every two to three weeks is enough to keep the crown viable through a dormant August. That is far less than keeping it green.
What does it cost to stay green instead?
Broadly double the water across July and August, on the two months when restrictions are tightest and rates are highest.
Will it come back?
In a normal September, yes, within two to three weeks of cooler nights. Overseeding at the same time makes the recovery faster and thicker.

November blow-out

  1. Shut the main irrigation valve and open a drain or the lowest head
  2. Compressor at no more than 50 psi for rigid pipe, 80 psi for poly — higher will split fittings
  3. One zone at a time, never all at once, and never run a zone dry for more than about two minutes
  4. Backflow preventer drained and its test cocks left at 45 degrees
  5. Insulate the exposed backflow assembly
  6. Note the date. Two years running is how systems quietly get destroyed
