The year

What your yard needs, and when.

Scroll sideways, or tab into the calendar and use the arrow keys. On a phone it stacks.

  1. Jan

    January

    No spend

    Nothing

    Frozen ground, dormant everything. Do not walk on a frosted lawn — the blades snap and you will see the footprints in April.

  2. Feb

    February

    Light

    Structural pruning

    Dormant pruning on deciduous trees and shrubs, while the framework is visible. The one month the shape of a garden is honest.

  3. Mar

    March

    Peak

    Clean-up and first cut

    Cut back perennials, clear beds, first mow high. Pre-emergent goes down now or not at all.

  4. Apr

    April

    Peak

    Planting and irrigation start-up

    Best planting window here. Charge the irrigation, walk every head, fix what the winter broke.

  5. May

    May

    Steady

    Growth management

    Weekly mowing begins in earnest. First fertiliser if the soil test asked for it — not automatically.

  6. Jun

    June

    Steady

    Deep watering, less often

    Fewer, longer waterings drive roots down. Daily light watering in June is how you build a lawn that dies in August.

  7. Jul

    July

    Light

    Hold the line

    No fertiliser, no aggressive pruning. Raise the mower height and shade the roots. This month is about not making it worse.

  8. Aug

    August

    No spend

    Let it go dormant

    A brown lawn in a Boise August is dormant, not dead, and it greens back up. Watering it through peak restriction costs money and achieves very little.

  9. Sep

    September

    Peak

    The real growing month

    Aerate, overseed, fertilise. If you do one thing to a lawn all year, do it now — not in spring.

  10. Oct

    October

    Steady

    Planting and bulbs

    Second-best planting window; roots establish before frost. Bulbs go in.

  11. Nov

    November

    Steady

    Blow out and cut back

    Irrigation blow-out before the first hard freeze — the single most expensive thing to forget in this valley.

  12. Dec

    December

    No spend

    Nothing

    Leave the seed heads and the leaf litter in the beds. It is habitat, it is insulation, and tidying it is work you are paying for twice.

When not to spend money

The months we would rather you kept the cash.

Three of the twelve months on that calendar need nothing at all, and two more need very little. We are saying so on our own website, which should tell you what our maintenance quotes look like.

January and December

Genuinely nothing. Any company billing a full monthly rate across a Boise winter is charging you for a truck that is not coming.

August

Let the lawn go dormant. It is the single biggest saving available to most of our clients, and it costs us the summer watering work.

July

Hold, do not push. Fertiliser in July burns and fresh planting bakes. Spend the money in September instead.

Common questions

Before you call.

Why do you only bill nine months?

Because January, February and December have nothing in them that needs a scheduled visit. We stay on call for storm damage and we do dormant pruning in February when the weather allows, but charging a flat twelve-month rate for a nine-month season is just a loan you did not ask for.

My lawn goes brown in August. Is it dying?

Almost certainly not. Cool-season grass goes dormant in heat and greens back up when temperatures drop and the September rain arrives. Watering it through a peak-restriction August costs a lot and mostly grows weeds. If it is still brown in October, then we should talk.

When should I actually fertilise?

September, and then late October if at all. Spring fertiliser feels right and mostly feeds top growth you then have to mow. If someone is putting four spring applications on your lawn, ask what the soil test said — and whether there was one.

Do you do design as well as maintenance?

Yes, and we would rather do both, because a garden designed without knowing who maintains it usually gets maintained badly. Design consults are separate and paid; the fee comes off the install if you go ahead with us.

What about native and low-water planting?

It is most of what we install now. It is not zero-maintenance — that is the myth — but after two establishment seasons it needs a fraction of the water and about a third of the labour of turf.

Can you take over a garden someone else designed?

Happily, and we will not disparage the previous work to win the job. We will tell you honestly what is thriving, what is in the wrong place, and what is going to be a problem in three years.
