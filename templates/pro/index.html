What it takes to put one online

Nothing here gets compiled, and nothing sits running on a server waiting for a request. Four steps, and the first three you only do once.

  1. Pick a host

    Netlify, Vercel, Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages all have a free tier that serves a static site. Any ordinary shared host with an FTP upload works too.

  2. Upload the folder

    Unzip the static build and drag the whole folder into the host's deploy box. There is no build command to configure and no server to provision.

  3. Point your domain at it

    Add the domain in the host's dashboard and update the DNS record it gives you. HTTPS is issued automatically on all four hosts above.

  4. Edit the text

    Open index.html in any editor, change the words between the tags, and re-upload. Nothing has to be recompiled for a copy change.

Which edition you actually need

Most people should start free. These are the cases where paying is the right call.

You need something online this week

Take the free edition of whichever template matches your trade, change the text, and ship it. You can buy the Pro edition later if you outgrow it — nothing is wasted.

You want it to look like your business, not the demo

Buy the Pro edition. The Astro source is where colours, type and section order actually live; the built HTML can only be edited so far before it fights you.

You are building it for someone else

Pro edition, for the licence rather than the files. It is what permits client work and removes the attribution line.

You are not sure the layout suits you

Open the live demo on your phone before you decide. Every template on this page has one, and that is the whole point of them being free to look at.

Questions worth asking first

What is the difference between the free template and the $19 Pro edition?

The free edition is the built site under an MIT licence, which is enough to launch. The Pro edition adds the Astro source so you can change the design rather than only the words, a commercial licence with no attribution requirement, and on some templates a set of photographs the free build does not include.

I am not a developer. Can I still use one of these?

If you can unzip a folder and drag it onto a hosting service, you can put the free or Pro static build online. Editing text and prices means opening one HTML file and typing between the tags. Changing layout or colours is where the Astro source, and some comfort with code, starts to matter.

Why would I buy a template instead of using Squarespace or Wix?

Different trade-off, and the honest answer is that it is not always the right one. These are a one-off payment with no monthly fee and no platform to be locked into, and they are fast because they are plain static files. What you give up is the visual editor, the built-in booking and payment integrations, and the hosting being handled for you.

Can I hand this to a client and charge for the work?

Yes — that is the case the Pro licence is written for. It covers unlimited personal and commercial projects including client sites, with no credit required anywhere on the page. The one thing you cannot do is resell the template itself as a template, theme or starter kit.

Are the photographs in the demos free to use?

The photography in the Pro builds is licensed stock, included so the site looks finished on day one rather than as final artwork. Replace it with your own — a real photo of your room, your team or your work will outperform any stock image on a local business site.

What do I get after buying, and what happens when the template is updated?

Two downloads: the static build and the Astro source. The licence includes updates to that template and email support; you re-download from the same Gumroad link, so keep the receipt.

Can I move it to WordPress or another platform later?

The content is yours and the markup is standard HTML, so the text and images port anywhere. The template itself does not convert — it is a static site, not a WordPress theme, and rebuilding it as one is a separate job.
