Safety Protocol.

The demo explains how each pose is built. This is the rest of it — including six questions worth asking any newborn photographer, with the answers that should reassure you and the ones that should not. Print it and take it to a consultation somewhere else.

Ask any photographer

Questions for a newborn photographer with good and poor answers
AskA good answerA poor one
How is the froggy pose achieved? A composite of two frames with hands supporting the head in each. Any answer that describes the baby holding their own head, or no answer at all.
Is anything ever suspended or unsupported? No. Slings, baskets and buckets are at floor level, weighted, and held. "It is perfectly safe" without describing the support.
Who is the spotter? A named person, usually a parent, within arm's reach for every non-flat frame. The photographer alone, or a spotter only "for the tricky ones".
What temperature is the room? 26 to 28°C, and they warn you to dress lightly. They have not thought about it.
What happens if someone is ill? Free reschedule, no discussion, including if the photographer is ill. A fee, or a shrug.
Is your infant CPR current? Yes, with a date. Anything vague. It is a fair question and nobody competent minds it.

The composite, step by step

  1. 1 Frame and light the empty set first Every second the baby is in the set should be a second you are photographing, not adjusting.
  2. 2 Shoot the empty prop or background plate This is the frame the hands are eventually blended into. Shooting it first means the light matches exactly.
  3. 3 Place the baby with the spotter supporting Head and neck first, always. The spotter does not let go between frames.
  4. 4 Shoot with support on one side One hand visible, one side of the pose clean.
  5. 5 Shoot with support on the other side The mirror frame. Between them, every part of the pose has been photographed unobstructed.
  6. 6 Blend the clean halves onto the plate The result is a picture of a pose that existed only with hands in it. That is the honest description of the technique.

Session checklist

  • ☐ Room to 26–28°C an hour before arrival; thermometer visible, not guessed at.
  • ☐ Hands washed and sanitised; again between every setup.
  • ☐ All props load-tested with a weight before the baby is anywhere near them.
  • ☐ Buckets and baskets weighted and placed on the floor, never on a surface.
  • ☐ Spotter briefed by name before the first frame, not recruited mid-pose.
  • ☐ Nothing above floor level. If a prop needs height, the height is composited.
  • ☐ Baby is never woken into a pose; limbs are never straightened or turned against resistance.
  • ☐ Session stops the moment the baby is unsettled, and resumes when they are not.

Five red flags

  1. 1 A portfolio of unsupported poses with no explanation of how they were made.
  2. 2 A studio that discourages a parent from staying in the room.
  3. 3 A cold room, or one where nobody mentions temperature at all.
  4. 4 Props balanced on tables, chairs, or anything above floor level.
  5. 5 Pressure to book inside a "window" with a deposit that does not move.

General guidance from working practice, not medical advice. Where your midwife, health visitor or paediatrician says otherwise, they are right and this page is not.
