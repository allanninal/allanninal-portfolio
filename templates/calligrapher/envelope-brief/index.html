Envelope brief.

Every addressing job that went wrong in fourteen years went wrong in one of three places: the list, the stock, or the count. This is the sheet that settles all three before a date is held, and it is the reason I have never re-written a batch at my own cost.

The list, in five columns

One column per line, in the order it appears on the envelope. I retype nothing and normalise nothing — a guessed surname is worse than a copied one, and the couple is the only person who knows which spelling is the right one.

Send it as a spreadsheet, not a PDF and not a photograph of a notebook
ColumnWhat goes in it
A — Line 1Exactly as it should appear. “Dr and Mrs Whitfield Ames”, not “ames, w”. Whatever is in this cell is what goes on the envelope.
B — Line 2Street, or “and family”, or blank. Blank cells are honoured, not filled in.
C — Line 3City, state, ZIP as one line. Two if your stock is narrow — tell me which and it stays consistent across all two hundred.
D — Line 4Country, for the six international ones. Left blank domestically rather than written “USA”.
E — NotesAnything I must not normalise: a title that looks wrong and is right, a spelling that is theirs.

The test, in five minutes

One envelope, both inks, both nibs, written and left for an hour. What it tells you:

  • — Laser-printed or coated envelopes: ink beads and will not dry → Gouache, or a different envelope. The toner is plastic and nothing water-based sticks to it.
  • — Cotton and handmade paper: feathers along the fibre → A less free-flowing ink, a finer nib, and a test on the spare sheet before the first real one. Beautiful stock, and it is the reason spares exist.
  • — Dark or black stock: ink disappears into it → White gouache mixed to single cream, remixed every twenty minutes because it settles. Costs about a third more in time and I quote it that way.
  • — Vellum, glassine, translucent: rejects water-based ink entirely → Gouache again, or an entirely different plan. Test first — this is the one that surprises people who have chosen a beautiful vellum wrap.
  • — Metallic and pearlescent finishes: ink sits on the coating and rubs off → Sand the coating (no), or accept a slightly heavier gouache line. Always tested, never assumed.
