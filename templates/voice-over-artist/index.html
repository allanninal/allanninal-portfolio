Term multipliers · base is twelve months from first air date

Forever is a multiple, not a rounding.

  1. 0.6× 13 weeks A seasonal or test flight. Cheaper because it ends, and it has to actually end.
  2. 1× 12 months The standard term everything on this page is priced against.
  3. 1.7× 24 months Not double: the second year is worth less than the first to almost every campaign.
  4. 3.5× Perpetuity Forever is a multiple, not a rounding. It also means the recording can outlive the product, the agency and me.

Month thirteen is agreed before the session, not discovered during it. Renewal is the same licence fee less 15%, quoted in the original confirmation, and it holds for sixty days after the term ends. This is the single most common dispute in the trade and it is entirely avoidable by writing one sentence down in advance.

One word, four prices

“Buy-out” is not a licence.

Four incompatible meanings of one word, all in current use, all written into briefs as though they were interchangeable. I quote against the first three and decline the fourth, which costs me a job every few months and has never once cost me an argument.

All media, worldwide, in perpetuity
The maximum. Everything, everywhere, forever. Roughly ten times a single-medium national year, and it is the only reading of the word that genuinely buys the recording outright.
All media, one territory, fixed term
Common, reasonable, and about a third of the above. This is what most agencies actually mean when they write "full buy-out" into a brief.
One medium, unlimited term
Perpetual radio, say, and nothing else. Cheap now, and the reason a ten-year-old spot occasionally reappears with nobody left who remembers agreeing to it.
Whatever the client decides later
Not a licence. A brief that says "buy-out" without naming media, territory and term has named a price for an unspecified permission, and every unspecified permission resolves in one direction.

Reels

Four reels, and what each one is for.

  • Commercial 1:04 Six spots, warm and unhurried. The register most brands ask for and the one that dates fastest.
  • Narration 1:38 Long-form documentary and corporate. Paced for someone who is going to listen for forty minutes, not four seconds.
  • E-learning 2:10 Instructional, with the pauses left in. Compliance modules are the least glamorous and most reliable work in this trade.
  • Explainer 0:52 Faster, brighter, more consonant. Written to survive being played without sound and then replayed with it.

Three things I decline.

  • Synthetic voice training — No recording of mine is licensed as training data for a voice model, and every contract I sign says so in a sentence I wrote. This is the live question in the trade and a site that will not answer it has answered it.
  • Gambling and payday lending — A personal line rather than an industry one. Stated here so nobody spends an afternoon on a brief I am going to return.
  • Unnamed usage — A brief that will not say where the audio runs is asking for a price without a product. I will quote the moment media, territory and term exist.

Common questions

Mostly about the second half of the invoice.

Why not just charge by the hour?

Because the hour is not what is being sold. A forty-five-second read takes about the same time to record whether it runs in one building or on national television, and an hourly rate prices those identically. It would mean charging the internal training buyer too much and the national campaign far too little.

What does the session fee actually cover?

The recording itself: direction, the read, editing, and one round of pickups if the script changes after the fact. Delivery is broadcast-ready WAV plus MP3. It does not cover permission for the recording to be used anywhere, which is the separate half of every quote on this page.

We only want a buy-out. What does that cost?

It depends entirely on which of the four things on this page you mean by the word. Name the media, the territory and the term, and the number is on the matrix. A brief that says "buy-out" and nothing else is asking me to guess at a permission and then be bound by the guess.

What happens in month thirteen?

The renewal figure is in your original confirmation email: the same licence less 15%, held for sixty days after the term ends. Nothing goes up because the campaign worked. The point of agreeing it before the session is that neither of us has to have that conversation while the spot is still on air.

Can we use the audio somewhere it was not licensed for?

Ask first and it is almost always a small addition to the existing licence rather than a new quote. The expensive version is the one discovered eighteen months later, because by then it is a rights conversation rather than an invoice.

Do you take direction live?

Source-Connect, Zoom or a phone patch, all included in the session fee. Most sessions do not need it. The ones that do are usually scripts where somebody has not yet decided what the line means, and that is worth twenty minutes of everyone being in the same room.
