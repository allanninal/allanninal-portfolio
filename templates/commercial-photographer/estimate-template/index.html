Estimate Template.

The demo shows one worked estimate and the calculator behind it. This is the blank instrument — what an estimate needs in it, the clauses that decide arguments months later, and what a client should ask before signing one. Including of us.

What an estimate needs

Sections of a commercial photography estimate
SectionWhat goes in it
HeaderStudio, client, project name, estimate number, date, and the date the estimate expires.
Creative feeShoot days and prep days, priced separately. One line each, never bundled.
ProductionCrew, studio, kit, props, travel. Every line a real cost, no "sundries".
Post-productionNumber of finished frames and the per-frame time assumed. This is where scope creeps.
LicenceMedia, term, territory, exclusivity — stated as four values, priced as one line.
AssumptionsWhat the price depends on: samples arriving by a date, one round of amends, a named approver.
TermsOverrun, cancellation, payment terms, and what happens to the licence if the invoice is unpaid.

Six clauses worth the argument now

Licence commences on first use
Without it the client can sit on the files for a year and then start a three-year term. Photographers lose whole years to this.
Rounds of amends, numbered
Two rounds is normal. Unnumbered amends is an open cheque written by the photographer.
Named approver
One person signs off. A shoot approved by committee is a shoot that gets re-approved.
Weather and postponement
Who carries the cost of a day that cannot happen. On location this is the single most expensive ambiguity.
Licence suspended until paid
The only leverage a photographer has. It is also fair: usage is what was sold, so usage is what is withheld.
Credit, where applicable
Not always possible in advertising. Worth asking for in editorial and worth waiving knowingly rather than silently.

What a client should ask

Of any studio, ours included.

  1. 1 Ask for the licence to be stated as four values — media, term, territory, exclusivity. If any is missing, the estimate is incomplete.
  2. 2 Ask what happens if you need to extend. A renewal quoted now is cheaper than a renegotiation later.
  3. 3 Ask how many finished frames are included and what a frame beyond that costs.
  4. 4 Ask what the estimate assumes. The assumptions section is where a cheap estimate hides its expensive surprises.
  5. 5 Ask whether the day rate includes prep. If it does, the photographer is either absorbing it or not doing it.

Four expensive habits

Common client mistakes and what to do instead
Instead ofDo this
Buying perpetual worldwide by defaultBuy the term you can actually foresee. Renewal is quoted at the same rate, not at a penalty.
Treating retouching as a rounding errorIt is often the second-largest line. Decide the number of finals before the shoot, not after.
Asking to "buy the copyright"Almost always the wrong instrument. Describe the usage you need and let the licence cover it.
Comparing day rates between studiosCompare total estimates for identical usage. A low day rate with production buried in it is not cheaper.

General guidance for commercial practice, not legal advice. Licensing conventions vary by market and your contract is the authority.
