How It Goes

Nobody opens a tin until you have lived with it.

  1. 1

    We come and look at the light

    Not just the rooms. Which way they face, what time you use them, and what is already in there. Colour behaves differently in every one of those, and this is the visit that prevents the expensive mistake.

  2. 2

    Samples on your walls

    Three to five candidates per space, painted large on more than one elevation and left up for two days. You live with them before anyone opens a real tin.

  3. 3

    A quote that itemises the prep

    Filling, sanding, caulk replacement, priming and any repairs, priced as their own lines. If a cheaper quote exists, this is the section it left out.

  4. 4

    The work, and the evenings

    Furniture moved and covered, floors protected, and the site put back every evening. You should be able to use the rooms we are not in.

  5. 5

    Walk it in daylight

    We walk the job with you in daylight rather than under work lamps, snag anything either of us finds, then issue the 2-year workmanship warranty in writing.

About the Owner

Hi, I'm Mira Halden.

I trained as a scenic painter for theatre before I ever painted a house, which is where I learned that colour is a lighting problem more than a taste problem. A grey that reads soft in a showroom goes green in a north-facing room at four in the afternoon. That is the single most common reason people end up hating a colour they chose carefully, and it is why we paint large samples on your actual walls and leave them up for two days before anyone opens a real tin. Eleven years, seven people, and I still do the colour consultations myself.

My commitment is straightforward: give you an honest assessment, quote you a flat price before we touch anything, do the work to code with the proper permit, and leave your home cleaner than we found it. Every technician I employ is licensed, background-checked, and trained under my direct supervision.

We are seven people, all employed, and I do the colour consultations myself. Nobody gets handed to a subcontractor halfway through, and the person who prepped your walls is the person who paints them.

How do I stop choosing a colour I end up hating?
Stop choosing from chips. A chip is two inches square, viewed under shop lighting, next to fifty other colours — three things that all distort it. Paint large samples on the actual wall, on more than one elevation, and look at them at the times of day you actually use the room. Almost every colour regret we get called about was chosen from a chip.
Why is one quote thousands cheaper than another?
Almost always the preparation. Filling, sanding, caulk replacement and priming are invisible in the finished job and account for most of the hours. A quote with one line that says "prep" and a quote with nine lines are describing different jobs, and you find out which in the second winter.
Can you paint over wallpaper?
We do not. The paint re-wets the adhesive, the seams telegraph through, and bubbles appear within a year. Removing it properly costs more up front and less over any period longer than about eighteen months.
Do I need to move out?
No. We work room by room, cover everything, and put the site back each evening, so you keep the rooms we are not in. Cabinet spraying is the exception — the kitchen is out of use for a few days.
What is the best time of year for exterior work in Rhode Island?
Late spring through early autumn. Modern acrylics go down to about 50°F, but the surface has to be dry through, and after a wet spell that takes longer than people expect. We would rather push a week than paint over damp clapboard.
How long does it take?
A single room is usually two to three days including prep and drying. A full interior runs one to two weeks. A full exterior on a typical house is two weeks, weather permitting, and weather is the variable we cannot promise around.
Do you use the paint I have already bought?
Yes, though we will tell you honestly if it is the wrong product for the surface. Using a wall paint on trim, or an interior product outside, is a job that fails early no matter how well it goes on.
What does the warranty cover?
Two years on our workmanship: peeling, flaking, blistering or failure caused by how it was applied or prepared. Fading and the paint’s own performance sit under the manufacturer’s warranty, which we register in your name.
