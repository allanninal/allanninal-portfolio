The decoder

Nine words that cost people money.

None of these are tricks. They are all standard, regulated terms — which is exactly why nobody questions them.

Valuation
Sounds like Insurance.
Actually means It is not insurance. It is the mover’s declared limit of liability, regulated by the DOT rather than by an insurer.
What it costs you The free default pays 60¢ per pound. Actual insurance is a separate product you buy elsewhere.
Released value protection
Sounds like Included protection, at no charge.
Actually means The federally-required free minimum. It pays 60 cents per pound per article, regardless of what the article is worth.
What it costs you A 40 lb television pays out $24. A 6 lb laptop pays out $3.60.
Full value protection
Sounds like The expensive upgrade.
Actually means The mover must repair, replace or pay cash value. The floor is $6 per pound of shipment weight.
What it costs you Typically 1–2% of declared value. On most moves it is the difference between a claim and a shrug.
Binding estimate
Sounds like A quote.
Actually means A fixed price for the services and items listed. It does not move unless you change the list.
What it costs you Nothing extra — but read what is on the list, because anything not on it is not in the price.
Non-binding estimate
Sounds like A quote.
Actually means An informed guess. The final price is based on actual weight and can legally exceed the estimate.
What it costs you You must be able to pay 110% of it at delivery. The rest can be billed within 30 days.
Binding not-to-exceed
Sounds like Complicated.
Actually means The best of the three. You pay the lower of the estimate or the actual weight.
What it costs you Nothing. If a mover offers this one, that is a good sign.
Accessorial charges
Sounds like Small extras.
Actually means Anything beyond loading, driving and unloading — stairs, long carry, shuttle, packing, bulky items.
What it costs you Routinely several hundred dollars, and the single most common source of invoice shock.
Long carry
Sounds like A short walk.
Actually means Distance from the truck to your door beyond an included allowance, often 75 feet.
What it costs you Charged per 50 or 75 feet. Ask what the allowance is before move day, not on it.
Shuttle service
Sounds like A convenience.
Actually means A smaller truck used because the trailer physically cannot reach your street.
What it costs you One of the largest single accessorials. If your street is narrow, raise it at survey.

Common questions

Before you book.

Is valuation the same as insurance?

No, and this is the single most common misunderstanding in the trade. Valuation is the mover’s declared limit of liability under DOT rules. Insurance is a separate product from an insurer. If you want true insurance you buy it separately — ask us and we will tell you who does it locally.

What does 60 cents a pound actually mean?

It means a 40 lb television is covered for $24. Released value protection is free and required by federal law, so it is what you get by default. It is not a scam, it is a floor — but almost nobody realises it is what they accepted.

Which estimate type should I ask for?

Binding not-to-exceed, every time. You pay the lower of the estimate or the actual weight, so the risk sits with us rather than you. If a mover will not offer it, ask why.

Why does a survey matter?

Long-distance price is weight, and nobody estimates weight accurately over the phone. A survey also catches the things that become accessorials — narrow streets, stairs, a long carry — while there is still time to price them honestly.

Can the price go up on move day?

On a binding estimate, only if you add items or services. On a non-binding one, yes — legally it can, and you must be able to pay 110% at delivery. That difference is why the estimate type matters more than the headline number.

What is the deposit situation?

We take none for local moves and a small one for interstate. A mover demanding a large cash deposit up front is a recognised warning sign; the FMCSA publishes guidance on exactly this at protectyourmove.gov.
