Session Kit.

The demo publishes what can and cannot be asked of an animal. This is the kit that makes the short list happen — the sounds, the order to spend them in, and the two sessions that need their own method.

Sounds, and what each buys

Sounds used in a pet session, what each produces and when to use it
SoundWhat it getsWhen to spend it
A squeaker, once Ears hard forward, eyes to the source Late. It is the strongest thing in the bag and it works once.
A kissy noise A soft look, sometimes a head tilt Early and often. It is the cheapest and the least startling.
A whistle, quiet A head tilt from most dogs Middle. Reliable twice, rarely three times.
Crinkling a wrapper Full attention from food-motivated dogs Late, and never near a resource-guarding dog.
Their own name A look, then a look away Almost never. It is the owner's word and burning it in a studio is rude.
Silence A settled, calm frame The last fifteen minutes. Most people never try it.
  1. 1 Nothing at all for the first fifteen minutes. Let the room become boring.
  2. 2 Cheapest sounds first — kissy noises, soft talking. These recharge; the others do not.
  3. 3 One new sound at a time, and only when the light and the frame are already right.
  4. 4 Never repeat a sound that just produced a good frame. You have the frame.
  5. 5 Squeaker last, if at all. Once it is spent the session is effectively over for alert frames.

Home visits

One room, chosen in advance
Whichever gets the most daylight before noon. Wandering the house loses twenty minutes.
A window, and no other light
Household lamps mix colour temperatures and turn a dog green. We turn them off.
Floors that are not slippery
A dog sliding on laminate will not settle. A rug solves it.
Other animals elsewhere
Unless they are in the pictures. An audience changes the behaviour of everyone.
Their own bed or blanket
A familiar surface is worth more than any prop and photographs better than a studio one.
Nobody new in the room
Two people maximum, both known to the animal. I am already the stranger.

Running a senior session

How to run a senior or end-of-life pet session
When Do Why
Before Ask what they can still do Stairs, standing, jumping onto furniture. Plan the session around what is comfortable rather than discovering it.
Arrival Sit on the floor, no camera, ten minutes An old dog will not adjust to a stranger quickly, and there is no version of this worth rushing.
First Photograph them where they already are Do not move an old dog for a better background. Move the background.
Middle Hands, feet, grey muzzles, the collar The details are the pictures families come back to. Ask for the hand on the head; most people are too polite to.
If tired Stop There is no frame worth the last twenty minutes of a tired old dog. We will come back if it matters.
After Deliver in three days Families booking these are often working to a horizon. Three weeks is the wrong answer.

Lighting a black coat

  • —Light the background separately from the animal. One source cannot do both.
  • —Rim from behind at 45 degrees. It is what draws an edge out of a dark ground.
  • —Expose for the eye, not for the coat. The eye is the only thing that must be right.
  • —A background one to two stops away from the coat — lighter or darker, never equal.
  • —Accept that some detail is gone. A black dog rendered as shape and eye is a portrait, not a failure.

Working practice, not veterinary advice. If an animal is unwell, the session waits.
