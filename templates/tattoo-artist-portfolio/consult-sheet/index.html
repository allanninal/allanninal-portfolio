Pro edition

Consult Sheet.

Eight things written down before the stencil goes on, and the zone table already filled in so nothing has to be remembered mid-consultation. Every dispute I have ever had with a client was about something on this sheet that neither of us had written down.

Before the stencil

  1. Placement, named not pointed atWritten down as one of the eight zones, because "on my arm" covers three of them with three different floors.
  2. Longest edge, in millimetresNot "small". Small is the word that produces a design under the floor, and it is always the client and the artist meaning two different things.
  3. Thinnest line in the drawingMeasured, against the floor for that zone. If it is under, the design is redrawn now rather than argued about at the stencil.
  4. Tightest gap in the drawingThe number that actually decides whether it is still readable at ten years, and the one nobody checks.
  5. Existing work within 50 mmSpacing, weight and whether it is healed. A new piece 20 mm from a four-year-old one has to be drawn against what that one has become, not what it was.
  6. Skin notesScarring, keloid history, moles inside the area, recent sunburn, and whether they are on anything that thins the blood. Any of these moves the plan.
  7. Session cap for the zoneFrom the table below. Agreed out loud before we start, so that stopping is a plan rather than a failure.
  8. Aftercare handed over, on paperAnd the six-week photograph asked for, in writing, because verbal is worth nothing at week seven.

At the stencil, out loud

“This is the last moment it can move. Stand up, sit down, look in the mirror, and look at it in a photograph on your own phone — the mirror flips it and your phone does not. I have moved a stencil eleven times for one client. I have never once regretted moving it and I have twice regretted not.”

Iona Petrakis
