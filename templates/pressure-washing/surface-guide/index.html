Soft wash, in order

  1. Pre-wet everything

    Surface and all surrounding planting. A dry surface absorbs solution unevenly and a dry plant absorbs it entirely.

  2. Apply low and work up

    Bottom to top on a vertical surface, so runs do not streak into dry areas.

  3. Dwell 5–10 minutes

    Long enough to kill, short enough not to dry. Never let solution dry on a surface — that is what causes streaking, not the solution itself.

  4. Re-wet if it is drying

    On a hot wall in direct sun, mist again rather than pushing on.

  5. Rinse top down, low pressure

    Gravity does the work. Rinse until the runoff is clear, then once more.

  6. Rinse the plants again

    Twice. The second rinse is the one people skip and the one that saves the shrubs.

Plant protection

  • ✓ Pre-wet every plant inside the splash radius, not just the ones directly below
  • ✓ Tarp anything precious, and weight the tarp — wind lifts it exactly when you stop watching
  • ✓ Never mix solution stronger than the label because the growth is stubborn; use dwell time instead
  • ✓ Rinse plants during the job as well as after, if it takes more than about twenty minutes
  • ✓ Downspouts route to somewhere — know where before you start, especially near a pond

Cold weather

How cold is too cold?
Below about 40°F the sodium hypochlorite loses effectiveness and anything you rinse can freeze on a walkway. That is both a bad clean and a liability.
What about the machine?
Drain the pump and lines or use pump antifreeze. A cracked pump head is a several-hundred-dollar lesson that everyone in this trade learns exactly once.
Is winter a bad time to book?
It is a good time to book and a bad time to schedule. Quote in winter, clean in spring, and get the early slot before the algae returns.
