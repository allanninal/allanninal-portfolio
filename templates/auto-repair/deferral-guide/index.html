Deferral Guide.

The demo page publishes one car's declines and how each turned out. This is the general form of the same question. Thirteen recommendations, sorted by what it costs to be wrong about them — 4 of which are not deferrable at all.

Four answers

Not deferrable
Stop driving it or book it this week.
Weeks, not months
It is degrading and the repair grows.
Watch it
Real, not yet costing anything. Re-measure next service.
Cosmetic
Fix it when you feel like it, or never.

And when the answer changes

The third column is the useful one. Everything on this list is deferrable right up until the moment named there.

Common recommendations, how deferrable each is, and what changes the answer
RecommendationAnswerWhat changes it
Brake pads under 3mm Not deferrable Below 2mm the rotor scores and the job roughly doubles.
Brake fluid dark or over 3% water Weeks, not months Boiling point falls. It fails when you need it most, which is the whole problem.
Tyre at the wear bars Not deferrable Wet stopping distance is already compromised at 3mm, well before the legal limit.
Wheel bearing humming Weeks, not months Once it scores the hub, the hub goes with it. See the ledger at 96,300 miles.
Oil seep, no drip Watch it When it drips onto hot exhaust it stops being a seep and starts being a smell and a fire risk.
Timing belt at interval Not deferrable On an interference engine there is no “slightly late”. It is the belt or the engine.
Struts failing a bounce test Watch it It becomes urgent when tyre wear turns uneven, because then it is eating tyres too.
Sway bar end link knock Watch it Noise long before it is a handling problem. Ours was three years early once.
Check engine light, steady Weeks, not months Steady means diagnose it. Flashing means stop — a flashing light is an active misfire dumping fuel into the converter.
Check engine light, flashing Not deferrable Nothing changes. It is already the expensive version.
Cabin air filter Cosmetic Nothing mechanical depends on it. It is comfort, and you can fit it yourself in ten minutes.
Serpentine belt with fine cracks Watch it Do it when something else already has the front of the engine apart. Labour overlap is the whole saving.
Clear-coat peel Cosmetic It spreads, and it never becomes mechanical. Purely how you feel about the car.

Five questions worth asking

A short list of services that appear on menus more often than they appear in owner manuals. None of these is always wrong — each is wrong often enough to be worth asking about.

Engine oil flush
Is this in the manufacturer schedule for my car?
Fuel injection service
What symptom is this treating, and did you measure it?
Transmission flush
Does my manufacturer specify a drain-and-fill instead? Many do, and a flush can be worse.
Coolant flush, early
What is the specified interval, and what did the test strip read?
Nitrogen tyre fill
What measurable difference does this make on a road car?

Before you agree to anything

  • ☐ Ask for the old part back. You are entitled to it, and asking changes the conversation.
  • ☐ Ask which labour guide the hours came from, and how many hours the operation is.
  • ☐ Ask what happens if it waits until the next service. A real answer is specific.
  • ☐ Ask whether the diagnostic fee comes off the repair. It should.
  • ☐ If two things share the same labour, ask what doing both together saves.

General guidance, not a substitute for your vehicle's own maintenance schedule. Where this page and your owner's manual disagree, the manual is the authority.
