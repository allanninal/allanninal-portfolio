The arithmetic

How the number is built

Every total on this page — the calculator above, the presets below, and the Pro Commission Quote Sheet — comes from the same seven steps, computed once in src/lib/quote.ts.

  1. Weigh the metal Grams, on a certified bench scale, before a single stone is set.
  2. Apply fineness Sterling 925, 9k 375, 14k 585, or 18k 750 — a legal fraction, not a rounding choice.
  3. Multiply by today's spot Gold or silver, read the day the quote is generated — see the observation date next to every total on this page.
  4. Add labour Hours on the bench × Kelham Bench's own stated rate — see the note below the calculator.
  5. Add stone cost As quoted for the specific stone — mined or lab-grown, disclosed either way.
  6. Add wastage A percentage of the metal cost — offcuts, filing dust and polishing loss that never make it into the finished piece.
  7. The total Metal + labour + stone + wastage (+ a hallmarking fee, if the piece clears the legal weight threshold below).

Not the jeweller's word

Hallmarks

The fineness figure in the calculator above isn't a marketing claim — it's a legal fact that, past a weight threshold, an independent Assay Office tests and strikes into the metal itself. In the UK any gold article over 1 g, silver over 7.78 g or platinum over 0.5 g must carry one before it can lawfully be sold as that metal. Kelham Bench's own sponsor's mark, IV, is registered at Sheffield.

Size, and the price gap

Stone reality

"1 carat" describes weight, not size — the face-up diameter below is what a carat actually looks like on a hand.

Lab-grown vs. mined, at the same grade

At 1 ct, G colour / VS2 clarity, a mined stone runs roughly $4,000.00–$8,000.00. A lab-grown stone at the same grade runs roughly $750.00–$1,500.00 — chemically, optically and physically the same material, at a fraction of the price.

Under the FTC Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23), a lab-grown stone must be disclosed as lab-grown at first mention — it is chemically, optically and physically the same material as a mined stone, at a fraction of the price, and it is not something a studio quietly upgrades a customer into.

Custom work

Commissions

Engagement and wedding pieces are commissioned, not stocked — the calculator above gives an indicative figure, and the final number is re-derived from that day's actual spot price when an order is placed. Every commission story on this page, like every piece of engagement copy, is written for a couple: a man and a woman.

  1. Consultation Metal, stone and sizing discussed together — the quote below is generated live, in the room or on a call.
  2. CAD or hand sketch A wax or CAD model approved before a single gram of metal is cut.
  3. Cast or fabricate Cast for most rings; hand-fabricated for anything with soldered joints or engraving that a cast can’t hold crisply.
  4. Set the stone Mined or lab-grown, disclosed either way — see the Stones section below.
  5. Hallmark Sent to Sheffield Assay Office if the piece clears the legal weight threshold — see the Hallmarks section.
  6. Polish & deliver Final polish, ring sizing checked once more, then collection or insured shipping. Realistically 4–6 weeks end to end.

FAQ

Questions

Can any ring be resized?

Most plain and light pavé bands, yes — a jeweller can usually move a size or two either way. Eternity bands set with stones all the way around, channel-set bands, and some tension settings cannot be resized without unsetting and resetting every stone, because there is no bare metal left to stretch or cut. Ask before you order if resizing matters to you.

What does a hallmark actually certify?

A hallmark is an independent Assay Office's own struck mark, registered against the maker's sponsor's mark — it certifies the metal's fineness was tested and found to match what's claimed, not a design or a maker's reputation. It is a third party's word, not the studio's.

Is lab-grown "real"?

Yes — a lab-grown diamond is chemically, optically and physically identical to a mined one; the difference is where the carbon crystallised, not what it is. See the Stones section for the price gap at the same grade, and the FTC disclosure rule this studio follows.
