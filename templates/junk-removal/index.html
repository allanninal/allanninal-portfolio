Where it actually goes

61% diverted. The national rate is 32.1%.

Our rate is roughly double the national municipal average, which is exactly the kind of claim you should be sceptical of. Ask any hauler for the breakdown by material rather than the headline number, and ask which yard or charity actually receives it.

Metal and appliances

What we will not take.

Every one of these has a correct route, and it is not a truck.

Hazardous waste
Paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals. It needs a household hazardous waste day, which Baltimore runs free.
Asbestos and anything suspected of it
Licensed abatement, not a hauler. Pre-1985 floor tile and pipe wrap in particular.
Medical and sharps
Regulated stream. Pharmacies and hospitals take these back.
Full fuel tanks, propane, batteries over car size
Explosion risk in a compacting truck. Empty and separate them and we will take the shells.
Anything still attached to the building
We are haulers, not demolition. Once it is loose, it is ours.

Questions.

How do I know what fraction my pile is?

You mostly cannot, and that is fine. Send a photo and we will tell you before we arrive. The truck bed is 16 feet by 8 feet by 5 feet tall, so a full load is about 640 cubic feet — but nobody thinks in cubic feet, which is why we price in quarters.

Is your 61% diversion rate real?

It is our own measured figure across last year, and you should be sceptical: the national municipal recycling rate is 32.1%, so we are claiming nearly double. That is why the breakdown by material is on this page. The metal and cardboard numbers do most of the work; mixed construction debris drags it down.

Will you donate my furniture?

If it is genuinely usable. The test is whether you would take it into your own home. A sofa with a torn cushion is donatable; a sofa that has been in a damp basement is not, and charities are quite firm about that because they pay to dispose of what they cannot use.

Can I just use city bulk pickup?

For a few items, yes, and it is free. We will say so on the phone. Where we earn the money is volume, stairs, heavy items and same-week timing.

Do you charge by weight?

No, by volume. Weight matters to us at the tip but it is not something you can estimate, and a per-pound price is how a $200 job becomes a $600 invoice.

What if it is more than you quoted?

We tell you before we load, not after. If the pile is bigger than the photo suggested, you get the new number and can decide. Nobody gets a surprise on the invoice.
