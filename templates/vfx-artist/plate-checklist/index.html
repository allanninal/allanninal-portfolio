Pro edition

Plate Checklist.

The demo publishes what a bad plate costs. This is the page that stops it happening — written to be read at speed by somebody who is already busy and does not work in post. Everything in the first table takes about 10 minutes in total, across a whole setup. That is the entire argument.

On the day

Seven things, and how long each one takes
ItemTimeHowWhy
Tracking markers 2 min Crosses of tape, 4–6 in frame, on flat areas that stay lit and never go behind the subject. A solved track is the difference between a shot that takes an afternoon and one that takes three days.
Clean plate 30 s Roll thirty seconds of the empty frame, same lens, same exposure, before or after the take. The only thing on any list that makes a shot CHEAPER. Removal work roughly halves.
Lens and camera data 45 s Focal length, T-stop, focus distance, height, tilt. Written down, once, per setup. Free on the day. Reverse-engineering it from the image is most of a day per setup.
Greenscreen lighting 5 min Even to within a stop, lit separately from the subject, subject at least 2 m off it. The single most common reason a key needs hand-drawn mattes. Five minutes of relight beats a week of roto.
A backlight on hair 1 min Any hard source behind the subject, off-axis. Nothing in post recovers hair detail that was never separated from the background.
Reference ball and chart 20 s Grey ball, chrome ball, colour chart, held in the plate for one second at the head. Lighting reference for anything CG. Twenty seconds, and it is the difference between matched and nearly matched.
A photo of the set 30 s Phone panorama from the camera position, plus one of the floor. Costs nothing, answers a hundred questions in week three, and no one has ever regretted taking it.

What to avoid, and what to do instead

  1. Handheld through a removal area Up to ×4 on the shot. This is the most expensive habit on any set.
    Instead

    Lock it off, or add markers and accept that we will track it.

  2. Motion blur across the thing being removed ×2, and the result is never quite clean.
    Instead

    A faster shutter for that take, or a second locked pass.

  3. Green bounce onto skin Spill suppression on skin is hand work and it always shows a little.
    Instead

    Move the subject off the screen, or flag it.

  4. Reflective props against green A chrome kettle in front of a greenscreen is its own shot, priced as one.
    Instead

    Dull it, angle it, or shoot it separately.

  5. Changing the lens mid-setup without saying A track solved against the wrong focal length fails subtly, which is worse than failing loudly.
    Instead

    Say it. That is all.

5 decisions somebody has to make

  1. 01

    Is this shot actually a VFX shot?

    Roughly a fifth of the shots that arrive as VFX are better solved by moving the camera a metre or dressing the set. Ask before the day, not after.

  2. 02

    Green, blue, or neither?

    Blue for anything with warm skin and green foliage; green for most else; and neither for a subject that could simply be shot against the real background and rotoscoped. Neither is often correct.

  3. 03

    Where does the shot stop?

    Agree the frame range before the take. "We will fix the whole thing" is not a spec and it is how a 40-frame job becomes a 400-frame one.

  4. 04

    Who owns continuity of the fix?

    If a sign is removed in one shot it has to be removed in every shot of that street. This is discovered late roughly every time nobody names an owner.

  5. 05

    Is anybody from post coming to the recce?

    One hour on a recce prevents more cost than any other hour in the schedule. It is also the first line cut from every budget.

6 things that go wrong without one

  1. 1 No clean plate, so every removal is painted frame by frame instead of copied from a still.
  2. 2 Handheld with no markers, and a three-day hand-track on a shot that was quoted as a day.
  3. 3 Greenscreen lit as one unit with the subject, producing a hot spot and a shadow in the same frame.
  4. 4 Lens data never recorded, so the CG element is matched by eye and never quite sits.
  5. 5 A removal agreed for one shot and forgotten for the eleven others on the same street.
  6. 6 The VFX supervisor cut from the recce to save a day rate, and the saving spent four times over in post.

Written for commercial and episodic work with a small VFX component. Feature and virtual production pipelines have their own capture requirements, several of which contradict items on this sheet — which is itself the argument for having somebody from post at the recce rather than a printed page.
