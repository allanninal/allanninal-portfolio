A typeface is 502 glyphs.

Every foundry site is a specimen, and every specimen shows the same ninety characters. The decision is made on the other four hundred: the Romanian comma-below, the Polish ł, stacked Vietnamese diacritics, tabular figures that line up, and the fi ligature. All of it is published below, including the languages these families only partly support.

What these families cannot do.

Three of the nine rows say Partial, and each one names the characters. A foundry that ships a checklist of language names with ticks beside them is claiming Romanian while shipping a cedilla, which is a real font rendering a real character that is the wrong one. Nobody ever complains — they just stop using it.

12 characters no specimen shows

You will notice the ones that are missing.

Twelve characters, rendered as text in this page rather than exported as an image. Select them, copy them, paste them into your own document — which is the only test that means anything and the one a PNG specimen quietly prevents.

ș S comma below Romanian. The cedilla version renders and is the wrong character.
ł L with stroke Polish. A separate drawing; an overlaid line collides at bold.
ế E circumflex acute Vietnamese. Two marks stacked, and about 130 more like it.
ﬁ fi ligature Without it, every fi in the family collides at text sizes.
İ Dotted capital I Turkish, with locl so a lowercase i keeps its dot in caps.
«» Guillemets French, Swiss and Russian quotes. Absent from most display faces.
¿ Inverted question Spanish. Needs its own drawing at display weights, not a rotation.
–— En and em dash Two widths, drawn separately. One dash used for both is a tell.
0 Slashed zero Quire Mono only. In a text face a slashed zero is a mistake.
½ True fraction Drawn, not two figures and a slash scaled down.
(H) Case-sensitive forms Parentheses and hyphens sit too low beside capitals unless there is an alternate.
1234 Tabular figures Equal widths so a column of numbers lines up. Four figure sets ship in every family.

Four families

Small, and finished.

Halvard Text

6 weights · 486 glyphs · Roman and italic, 6 weights

A workhorse for long reading at 9 to 12 pt. Drawn against newsprint first, which is why it holds up on a screen at 300% and looks slightly dull in a specimen.

Halvard Display

3 weights · 312 glyphs · Roman only, 3 weights

The same skeleton, tightened and sharpened for 40 pt and above. Smaller character set on purpose: a display face used for a Vietnamese paragraph is being used wrong.

Quire Mono

4 weights · 441 glyphs · Roman and oblique, 4 weights

For code and for tables. Zero is slashed, one has a foot, l has a tail, and the ambiguity budget was spent before anything else was drawn.

Bellrope Sans

8 weights · 502 glyphs · Roman and italic, 8 weights

The largest set here and the only one with full Vietnamese. Eight weights because it was drawn for an interface, and an interface needs a 450 that a print family never does.

Six stages · two months of them are two characters

How a family gets drawn.

  1. n and o, for a monthEverything else is derived from these two. A month sounds absurd and it is the reason the other 478 glyphs take six rather than twenty.
  2. The control charactersH O n o and a few more, spaced properly, at the size the family is actually for. Spacing before drawing, always — a beautifully drawn face with bad spacing is unusable and the reverse is merely plain.
  3. Lowercase, then capsIn that order, because the lowercase carries the texture and the caps have to fit into it rather than the other way round.
  4. Figures, four setsLining, oldstyle, tabular and proportional. Two hundred and forty drawings before a single accented character exists.
  5. Diacritics and the extended setWhere the glyph count actually goes. Polish, Czech, Turkish, Romanian, and the decision about Vietnamese, which is a hundred and thirty more per weight.
  6. Interpolation and testingWeights generated between the extremes, then every one of them checked by hand, because an interpolated 450 is wrong in a different way from an interpolated 700.

Common questions

Mostly about what is missing.

Why publish what the fonts cannot do?

Because the alternative is a checklist of language names with ticks beside them, which is how a font ends up claiming Romanian while shipping a cedilla instead of a comma-below. It renders, it is the wrong character, nobody complains, and they quietly stop using the family. Naming the two missing glyphs costs a line and saves the relationship.

Can I try the fonts?

Yes, free and unlimited, and the trial form on this page is separate from the commission form for exactly that reason. Trials are watermark-free and unlicensed for publication — they exist so you can set your own text, in your own language, and find out in ten minutes what a specimen cannot tell you.

What is actually in the download?

The static instances, the variable file where there is one, and the full character set as a PDF that lists every glyph rather than showing a pangram. No installer, no font manager, no account.

Why is Vietnamese only in one family?

Because it is roughly 130 extra drawings per weight, and in a four-family foundry that is a real decision rather than a checkbox. Bellrope Sans has it because it was drawn for an interface that needed it. Saying so is more useful than a tick in a table.

Do you draw Cyrillic?

No. A Cyrillic that has not been reviewed by somebody who reads Cyrillic is worse than no Cyrillic, and I do not read it. If a commission needs it I will bring in somebody who does and say so on the invoice.

Are the specimens on this site set in your fonts?

On this demo they are set in the template’s own faces, and this answer is here rather than buried because a foundry site that is coy about that is doing something worse. In the real thing you drop your own @font-face into the stylesheet and every waterfall and grid on the page becomes your family.
