Reference

Design system

Every ratio was computed with tools/a11y/contrast.py against all three grounds. This page is noindex: it documents the template for whoever builds with it.

A conventional shape, on purpose

Days 101–108 were portfolios that each argued with their own genre. Eight of those in a row is itself a house style, and this project's own divergence notes keep warning about exactly that. A landing page has a job, and a landing page that experiments with its own navigation is one that does not convert — so the shape here is a store page and the divergence comes from the section inventory instead.

The screenshots are drawn

A landing page is mostly screenshots and this game is fictional, so the four slots are SVG placeholders labelled with their ratio and the job each image has to do. That is the same call day 108 made and it is more useful here: somebody cloning this gets a brief in every slot rather than four stock photographs to delete.

Schema.org finally fits

Days 103–108 all had to settle for Person plus a Wikidata additionalType, because schema.org models no freelance creative discipline. This is a product, and VideoGame is a real type with a real offers block — so the price, the platforms, the languages and the publisher are all machine-readable rather than approximated.

Fit and support are never only a colour

The most useful section on the page is a list of reasons to skip the game, so getting that signalling right matters more here than anywhere. Both columns are headed in words (“Buy it if” / “Skip it if”), every row carries a glyph, and the accessibility table prints Yes, Partial or Not yet as text via a data-glyph ::before. --state-yes and --state-no are separate from the accent.

The no rows stay in

Two partials and one outright no in the accessibility table. A support list with nothing but ticks tells a player who needs one of those features nothing at all, because they cannot distinguish “supported” from “never checked”.
