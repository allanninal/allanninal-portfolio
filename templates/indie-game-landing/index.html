What it is

A lighthouse, a tide table, and one winter.

One island, one winter

You are the keeper. The light has to be lit every night from October to March, and every day the sea takes a little more of the path you used to walk to get there. Nothing chases you. The route just stops working.

The tide is the clock

There is no timer on screen and no stamina bar. There is a tide table, and it is accurate, and if you have not read it you will be on the wrong side of the causeway when it closes. Learning to read it is most of the game.

Nobody arrives to explain it

The story is in the logbook, the supply manifests and what is missing from them. There are no cutscenes and no voice-over. Some players finish without piecing it together, and that is a legitimate way to finish.

Before you buy

This game is not for everyone, and here is who.

Buy it if

  • +You liked a game where the map was the puzzle.
  • +You want something you can finish over a fortnight of evenings.
  • +You read the notes and the labels in games, and you are annoyed when there are none.
  • +You are happy for a game to be quiet for twenty minutes at a stretch.
  • +You want to be able to save and stop at any moment, because you have a life.

Skip it if

  • −You want combat. There is none, at all, and no amount of playing unlocks any.
  • −You want to be told what to do next. There is no quest marker and there will not be a patch that adds one.
  • −You bounce off games without a fail state. Nothing here can kill you and nothing can be lost permanently.
  • −You need eighty hours for your money. This is eleven, deliberately, and the price reflects it.
  • −You play mainly for multiplayer. It is single-player only, forever, and that is a design decision rather than a budget one.

Publishing the right-hand column is the highest-converting thing on this page, which sounds backwards and is not. It raises conversion among the people it does fit, it cuts refunds among the people it does not, and it is the clearest signal a developer can give that they know what they made.

Accessibility

6 supported. 3 not, and they are listed too.

What is supported at launch, including what is not
Feature Supported Note
Full subtitles, resizable Yes Three sizes, with an optional background box. Default is larger than most games ship.
Colourblind modes Yes Deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia. Nothing in the game is signalled by colour alone regardless.
Save anywhere, single button Yes Including mid-crossing. Stopping is never punished.
No fail state Yes Not an option — the design. You cannot die and nothing is permanently missable.
Full remapping, keyboard and pad Yes Every action, including the ones on modifiers.
Hold-to-press instead of hold Yes Every hold interaction has a toggle alternative.
Screen-reader support in menus Partial Menus and the logbook are read; in-world labels are not yet. This is the one we are least happy about.
One-handed play Partial Possible on a pad with remapping, awkward on keyboard. We have not tested it properly and will not claim it until we have.
Reduced motion Not yet The camera sway during crossings cannot currently be turned off. It is on the list, and until it ships this row says no.

Two partials and a no. Leaving them in is the point — a support list with nothing but ticks on it tells a player who needs one of these nothing at all, because they cannot tell the difference between "supported" and "never checked".

Common questions

Including what a wishlist actually does.

Is there a demo?

Yes, during Next Fest, and it is the first two in-game weeks rather than a bespoke slice. If the demo bores you the full game will too, which is exactly what a demo is for.

Why are there no screenshots on this page?

Because this is a website template and Lowtide is fictional. The four slots are drawn placeholders labelled with the ratio and the job each image has to do. If you are using this template, put your real captures there.

Eleven hours for $19?

Yes, and we would rather say so on the store page than have you find out after buying. An eleven-hour game you finish is worth more than an eighty-hour game you abandon at hour nine, but you are entitled to disagree before paying rather than after.

Will it come to consoles?

We would like it to and we have not signed anything, so the honest answer is that we do not know. We will not put a console logo on this page until a build exists.

Is it scary?

No, and it is not trying to be. It is a game about weather and being alone with a job to do. There is no threat and nothing jumps out.

What does a wishlist actually do?

It tells the store to show the game to more people at launch, and it emails you when it is out or on sale. That is all it does, it costs nothing, and it is genuinely the most useful thing you can do for a two-person studio.
