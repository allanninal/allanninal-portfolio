“Produced by” is four different jobs.

A producer and mix engineer in Memphis. The credit “produced by” covers everything from choosing a take to writing the song, so every record below says which of the four jobs I actually did — and the rate card charges for them separately, because they are separate work.

The four jobs

Different crafts, different rates.

WritingWrote
Split, not a fee
Melody, lyrics, chord changes — the song itself. This is the only one of the four that comes with a share of the publishing, and the only one where the credit follows the song forever rather than the recording.
ProducingProduced
Per song or per day
Arrangement, tempo, key, who plays, which take, what gets cut. The decisions about what the record IS. It is the job people mean least often and pay for most vaguely.
EngineeringEngineered
Per day
Mic choice and placement, gain, the routing, the headphone mixes, and keeping the session running so nobody is waiting on a cable. Invisible when it works.
MixingMixed
Per song
Balance, space, tone and automation across the finished multitrack. A separate craft with a separate ear, and the one most often bought as a standalone service.

An artist saying "I need a producer" means one of these four about as often as it means all of them. Working out which, before a rate is agreed, saves both sides the conversation where somebody expected a co-write and somebody else quoted for a mix.

Three points on one song, two hundred thousand streams

Three points is $2.85.

Producers are routinely offered royalty points instead of a fee, and almost nobody publishes what a point is worth. Here it is, end to end.

  1. 200,000 streams × $0.0035 $700 A mid-band per-stream rate. It varies by platform and by country and it is never the number in a press release.
  2. Master share to the label −$385 The recording side, before anybody downstream is paid. This split is the deal, not a law.
  3. Artist share of the rest −$220 The artist is paid before the producer, out of the same pool. That order is normal and worth knowing.
  4. Producer pool remaining $95 Everything the producer points are calculated against, for the whole song, for that year.
  5. 3 points of the pool $2.85 Not a typo, and not an unusual outcome for a song with two hundred thousand streams.

Points are worth taking when a record has a real chance of being big and worth nothing when it does not, which is most records including good ones. I take fees. If you would rather pay in points I will still do the work, at a reduced fee plus points, and I will show you this arithmetic first so that neither of us is confused later about what was agreed.

Before you send a mix

6 lines, and none of them slow you down.

The most common week of wasted work in this trade is a mix engineer waiting on a re-export. Six lines, and none of them takes longer than the email asking about them would.

Sample rate and depth
48 kHz / 24-bit
Whatever you tracked at, not converted. Send me the session rate and I will match it.
Start point
Bar 1, all files
Every stem the same length from the same zero. Consolidated, not trimmed to where the part happens to enter.
Headroom
−6 dB peak
Not because it sounds better, but because a stem clipped at export cannot be un-clipped at mix.
Master bus
Bypassed
No limiter, no bus compression, no "just a little" of anything. Send the mix bus chain as a note instead and I will listen to it.
Naming
01_Kick_In.wav
Number, source, mic or DI. Twenty files called Audio 1 through Audio 20 is an hour of somebody guessing.
Reference
One rough mix
Your rough, however bad. It tells me what you were hearing, which is information no stem carries.

Common questions

Mostly about the credit.

Why break the credit into four?

Because they are four crafts with four rates and an artist saying "I need a producer" means one of them about as often as all of them. Splitting it before a number is agreed prevents the conversation where one side expected a co-write and the other quoted for a mix.

Would you take points instead of a fee?

At a reduced fee plus points, yes. Instead of a fee, no — and the arithmetic on this page is why. Three points on two hundred thousand streams is under three dollars, and I would rather show you that up front than have it discovered eighteen months later.

Do you master as well?

No. I will recommend two people and I will happily be wrong about which of them suits your record. A mix engineer mastering their own mix loses the second pair of ears that is the entire point of the stage.

What if my stems are not to spec?

I will still take them, add an hour of prep, and tell you before I start rather than after. The spec exists to save you that hour, not to refuse work.

Can you work remotely?

For mixing, always — most of it is remote. For tracking and producing, at least the first day in the room, because the decisions that matter get made by people looking at each other.

Do you have a house sound?

Yes, and you should listen for it before hiring me rather than after. The discography is deliberately not sorted by how well the records did; it is sorted by year, which makes the pattern easier to hear.
