Every strike and hinge line above resolves to the same answer: a 3-inch screw that reaches the framing stud.

Reading the grade stamp

It is on the faceplate, on the edge of the door. If there is nothing there, that is itself the finding.

Grade 1
Commercial. 10 strikes, 250,000 cycles. Rare on residential doors.
Grade 2
Light commercial. 5 strikes, 150,000 cycles. The sensible residential target.
Grade 3
Residential builder-grade. 2 strikes, 100,000 cycles. Most US front doors.
No stamp
Untested, or not submitted for grading. Common on imported hardware.

Key control

A lock decides who can force the door. A keyway decides who can already open it.

Open keyway
Blanks sold anywhere. Anyone who has held the key can have copied it.
Restricted keyway
Blanks controlled by the manufacturer. Copies need proof of authority.
Patented keyway
Blank protected by patent. Legally enforceable key control until the patent lapses.
Master keyed
One key opens many. Convenient, and it enlarges what a lost key costs you.

After a lost key

  • ☐ Rekey rather than replace, unless the hardware is failing on its own merits.
  • ☐ Rekey every cylinder the lost key touched, including the garage pedestrian door.
  • ☐ If the key was on a fob with an address, treat it as a known address and do it the same day.
  • ☐ A master-keyed set means rekeying the whole set, not one door. Budget for it before you master-key.

General guidance, not a security assessment of your specific property. ANSI/BHMA grade definitions are published by the Builders Hardware Manufacturers Association.
