Slow, quiet, and rare. Television has this badly out of proportion.
Bumping
A cut key struck to jump the pins. Seconds, if the cylinder allows it.
Drilling
Loud and fast. Beaten by hardened inserts, not by grade.
Snapping
The euro-cylinder failure. Mostly a UK problem — named here because UK advice circulates widely.
Kick / spread
What actually happens. Read this column down before you read any row across.

6 of the 7 rows above are weak against a kick, and the one that is not is a strike plate rather than a lock.

The part nobody upsells

The eleven-dollar fix we would rather sell you than a cylinder.

The strike plate that came with your door is fastened with three-quarter-inch screws. Those bite the door casing — a thin trim board — and nothing behind it. A box strike with three-inch screws reaches past the casing into the framing stud, which is the first structural member in the wall.

  • $8–$14

    Box strike plate

    Wraps the bolt cavity instead of sitting flat on the jamb.

  • under $2

    3in wood screws, four

    Long enough to reach the stud behind the casing.

  • under $3

    Hinge screws, six

    The hinge side fails second. Same screws, same reason.

We do it for $45 on a call we are already making. It takes about twenty minutes.

This will not help a door with a rotted jamb, a glass sidelight within arm’s reach of the thumbturn, or an out-swinging door with exposed hinge pins. We will tell you if you have one of those instead of fitting a strike and taking the money.

Before you call anyone

Why the first quote you get is often not the price.

Locksmith bait-and-switch is documented enough that the FTC has published consumer guidance on it: a search ad quotes nineteen dollars, an unlicensed subcontractor arrives in an unmarked car, and the invoice at the door is three hundred and fifty. It is common enough that we lose calls to it.

  1. 1 Ask for the licence number before the van leaves. Ours is LOC-4471.
  2. 2 Ask what the call-out fee is and whether it is waived. Ours is $45, waived if we do the work.
  3. 3 Ask whether the person arriving is an employee. Ours are.
  4. 4 If the answer is “the technician will price it on site”, that is the scam.

Common questions

Before you call.

Is a Grade 1 deadbolt worth the money?

For the cylinder, yes — it resists drilling and survives far more cycles. For forced entry through the frame, it changes nothing. Fit the strike first, then buy the grade.

Are smart locks less secure?

Not usually less, but rarely more. The motorised bolt underneath is typically Grade 2 or 3. A smart lock changes who can open your door and when — a useful thing — but it does not change how hard the door is to force.

Should I worry about lock bumping?

Less than the internet suggests. It is real and it is fast, but it needs the right key blank and it leaves the lock working, so it is under-reported rather than common. Security pins defeat it, and any high-security cylinder has them.

Can you open my door without drilling it?

Almost always. Non-destructive entry is the default and it is what the call-out fee covers. If a lock genuinely has to be drilled we tell you before we start and the replacement is quoted first.

Do you charge more at night?

After 7pm the call-out is $70 instead of $45. The labour rate does not change. That is the whole difference and it is on this page so you do not hear it at the door.

How do I check a locksmith is licensed in Washington?

Ask for the endorsement number and the business name, then check it against the Department of Licensing record. Ours is LOC-4471, Redoubt Lock & Key. A firm that will not give you a number over the phone is telling you something.
