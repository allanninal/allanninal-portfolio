What each one does when it drifts

And the one that makes chlorine useless.

A pool can read 4 ppm chlorine and be effectively unsanitised. Row four is how.

What happens when each reading drifts out of band
DriftWhenWhat it doesFix
pH too low Below 7.2 Etches plaster, corrodes metal, stings eyes. Chlorine is aggressive but short-lived. Soda ash, slowly, then retest in 6 hours.
pH too high Above 7.8 Chlorine loses most of its bite, scale forms, water clouds. Muriatic acid in small doses. Check alkalinity first — high TA is usually the reason pH keeps climbing.
Alkalinity too high Above 120 ppm pH becomes stubborn and drifts upward no matter what you add. Acid additions with aeration. Slow work; nobody fixes this in one visit.
CYA too high Above 70 ppm The trap. Chlorine reads fine on a strip and is progressively less able to kill anything. This is how a pool with "normal" chlorine goes green. There is no chemical that removes it. Partial drain and refill is the only real answer — which is why we watch it rather than let it get there.
Calcium too low Below 200 ppm Water pulls calcium out of plaster and grout to satisfy itself. Calcium chloride. Easy to fix, easy to overshoot.
Calcium too high Above 500 ppm Scale on tile, heater elements and salt cells. Partial drain, or a sequestrant to buy time in hard-water areas like this one.

Common questions

Before you call.

My chlorine tests fine but the pool went green. How?

Almost always cyanuric acid. CYA stabilises chlorine against sunlight, but above roughly 70–100 ppm it also binds it so tightly that the chlorine cannot sanitise. A strip shows chlorine present and the water is effectively unprotected. It is the single most common cause of a green pool with "good" numbers, and no chemical removes CYA — you dilute it.

Do I actually need weekly service?

In an Arizona summer, most pools do. In winter, most do not. If you test and log yourself, a quarterly check is often enough — see the section above, which exists because we would rather have you at the right plan than the expensive one.

Are test strips good enough?

For chlorine and pH, roughly. For alkalinity and CYA they are poor, and CYA is the one that matters most. A drop kit is more work and enormously more accurate.

Why does my pH keep climbing?

Usually total alkalinity is too high, which makes pH stubborn and upward-drifting. Chasing pH with acid while ignoring TA is the most common mistake we see, and it burns through a lot of acid to no effect.

What is a green-pool recovery?

Filter clean, a controlled chlorine hold, brushing, and usually two or three visits over a week. Anyone promising a single visit is going to hand the pool back and let it re-bloom.

Do you handle salt systems?

Yes. A salt cell is a chlorine generator, not an alternative to chlorine — the same six numbers apply, and cells scale badly in hard water, so they get inspected on every visit.
