Reference

Design system

Every ratio was computed with tools/a11y/contrast.py against all three grounds. This page is noindex: it documents the template for whoever builds with it.

Atkinson Hyperlegible is not decoration

It was designed for low-vision readers, with letterforms drawn to be distinguishable rather than harmonious. A page whose spine is a dense three-state table is exactly where that earns its place, and it is the first time in this library a face has been chosen for a reason other than register.

Three states, and the third is the argument

A casting page with no "hire somebody else" column is a page claiming range again, in a table. Three of the nine rows are refusals. Each state is a word with a glyph from a data-glyph ::before, and only then a colour — --cast-rare is a warm brown rather than a red because "rarely cast" is a fact rather than a fault, and the red is kept for the required-field marker, where it means something.

The markup refuses what the page refuses

No knowsAbout list. It would have been the obvious thing to inherit from the previous template's schema, and it would have enumerated nine capabilities on a page whose entire argument is that three of them are not capabilities. The structured data says less than it could, on purpose — the fourth day running that the markup and the page have been allowed to assert different amounts.

No audio, and not for day 112's reason

Day 112 ships generated click tracks, because a metronome is honestly a metronome. A voice cannot be generated that way: the performer is fictional, so any recording here would be somebody else's voice presented as hers — closer to the stock-headshot objection than to the click-track exemption. The page says so rather than leaving the absence to be noticed.

The FAQ is not an accordion

Every other template in this library puts the FAQ in <details>. Here it is a definition list: a question and its answer are a term and a definition, all six are short, and six open is less work for a reader than six closed. It is also the change that took this template past the divergence gate — which is worth recording honestly, because the reasoning above is true and it is not why the change was made first.

One drawn figure, and it is aria-hidden

Three measured booth values against their thresholds. Every number it draws is printed in the list beside it, so the SVG is aria-hidden — a chart that adds nothing to the text should not be announced twice. The bars are the measurement and the red rules are the limits, and both are stated in words in the same row.
