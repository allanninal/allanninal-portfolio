Credit Standard.

The demo publishes a full block on every frame. This is the standard behind it — twelve lines, who supplies each, and the part nobody writes down: what to do when a title drops the block and the crew is the one that loses.

The block, in order

Lines of a fashion credit block, who supplies each and how it is written
LineSupplied byNote
Photography Photographer Always first. Studio name or individual, not both.
Styling Stylist Add "Assistant styling" as its own line if there was one. Assistants get credited.
Hair Hair stylist Separate from make-up unless genuinely one person, in which case "Hair and make-up".
Make-up Make-up artist Agency name after the person if they are represented.
Manicure Manicurist Dropped more often than any other line, and visible in more frames than people think.
Set design Set designer Include the builder if the set was built rather than dressed.
Casting Casting director Or the title, if they cast it in-house. Say which.
Production Producer The person who made the day happen. Frequently the photographer, and it still gets a line.
Digital tech Digital technician On any shoot tethered to a workstation.
Retouch Retoucher Including when it is the photographer. Especially then.
Model Model, with agency Format: Name at Agency. The agency is part of the credit, not decoration.
Garments Stylist or brand Designer names, or "Stylist’s own" where that is the truth.

Five rules

  1. 1 Order is fixed: image-makers, then subject, then garments. Do not reorder to put the photographer last out of modesty or first out of ego.
  2. 2 Everyone gets named, including assistants and including the retoucher when that is you.
  3. 3 The agency belongs with the model. "Name at Agency" is the format print uses.
  4. 4 Say "Stylist’s own" when garments were not lent. It is a real credit and it protects the stylist.
  5. 5 Nobody is credited who was not there. Padding a block is worse than a short one.

When a title drops it

Titles drop credit blocks — usually to save space, occasionally by policy, and the crew is the one that loses. What to do, in order:

  1. 1 Send the block again, formatted, in the same email as the files. Half of all drops are because nobody supplied it.
  2. 2 Ask once, politely, naming the specific lines missing. Do it before publication, when it costs the title nothing to fix.
  3. 3 If it publishes without them, post the frames yourself with the block intact. Your channels are the ones you control.
  4. 4 Do not withhold files over it. Punishing an editor punishes the crew you are trying to credit.
  5. 5 Remember it when the next commission comes. A title that will not credit a manicurist is telling you what a shoot day with them is like.

If you are commissioning

  • ☐Ask for the credit block with the delivery. A photographer who cannot produce one did not run a crew.
  • ☐Budget for the people on it. A block with nine names and a fee for one is a shoot somebody subsidised.
  • ☐Put the crediting requirement in the contract if the images go to a publication.
  • ☐If you are cutting a line from the block, cut it from the budget too, or say plainly that the work was unpaid.

Conventions vary between markets and titles. Where this page and a publication’s own style guide disagree, the publication wins — but ask before assuming it does.
