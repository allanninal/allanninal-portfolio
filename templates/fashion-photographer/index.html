Rates, said out loud

Editorial does not pay. That is not a complaint, it is the arithmetic.

Typical day rates by kind of fashion commission
Kind Day rate Note
Independent title $0 – $400 Expenses often unreimbursed. Everyone on the call sheet is there for the book.
Established title $500 – $1,500 A real fee. Still less than a commercial half-day.
Show coverage $1,200 – $2,000 Paid properly because the turnaround is overnight.
Lookbook $2,500 – $4,500 Plus licence, quoted separately by media, term and territory.
Campaign Quoted per job Where the licence usually costs more than the shoot.

So why do editorial at all?

Because it is how the book gets made, how you meet the stylists who recommend you, and how commercial clients find you. That is a working answer rather than a romantic one — and the shoot above that lost $830 is the reason three paid jobs happened.

Commissions

Five things in the first email.

What a commercial enquiry needs to say, so the first reply is a number rather than a question.

What is being shot
Garment count and whether there is a model changes the day more than anything else.
Where it runs
Lookbook, e-commerce, paid social and outdoor are four different licences.
How long you need it
A season, a year, or no end date. It is the cheapest thing to decide early.
Who is art directing
You, us, or an agency. All three are fine; not knowing on the day is not.
The date, honestly
Casting and crew are booked in the same week. A moving date costs the crew, not us.

Common questions

Before you write.

Why publish what a shoot paid?

Because the numbers are the only honest explanation for how this business is shaped. Editorial is subsidised by commercial work in every fashion studio in the country, and pretending otherwise leaves young photographers budgeting for a job that does not exist.

Will you credit everyone?

Always, on every image, wherever we control the caption. If a title drops the block we ask once and then we hold the line: we do not post the frame without the crew on it.

Do you shoot tests?

A few a year, with people we want to work with rather than to fill a day. A test where nobody is paid should at least be a test everybody chose.

Can you work with our art director?

Preferably. A strong deck makes the day faster and the results better. What slows a shoot is not direction, it is direction that arrives at eleven.

Who books the model?

Whoever is paying, usually. We will run casting if you want it and it is a line on the estimate — options, fittings and the agency booking fee, which is 20% on top of the model rate and surprises people every time.

Do you retouch in-house?

Small jobs yes, volume no. Kwabena is credited on the frames he worked on, which is the same reason everyone else is.
