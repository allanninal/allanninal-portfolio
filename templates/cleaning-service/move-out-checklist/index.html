Move-Out Inspection Checklist

Written from the tenant’s side, because that is who carries the risk. Print it, walk it, and photograph as you go.

The walk-through

Kitchen

  • ☐ Inside oven, including the door glass
  • ☐ Inside and under the fridge, seals wiped
  • ☐ Range hood filter degreased
  • ☐ Inside every cabinet and drawer
  • ☐ Sink drain and disposal, no odour

Bathrooms

  • ☐ Grout and silicone free of mould
  • ☐ Shower glass free of hard water
  • ☐ Extractor fan cover dust-free
  • ☐ Toilet base and fixings, not just the bowl
  • ☐ Inside the vanity

Living areas

  • ☐ Walls spot-cleaned, scuffs and hand marks
  • ☐ Skirting and door frames
  • ☐ Inside wardrobes and closets
  • ☐ Window tracks and sills
  • ☐ Blinds slat by slat

Whole property

  • ☐ Light fittings and switch plates
  • ☐ Carpet cleaned or professionally shampooed
  • ☐ All bulbs working
  • ☐ Bins emptied and washed
  • ☐ Keys, fobs and remotes accounted for

Where deposits actually go

The oven
The single most-cited deduction in end-of-tenancy disputes. Budget an hour or pay for it.
Carpet
A vacuum is not a clean. Many leases specify professional shampooing and name it as a condition.
Walls
Marks are cleaning; holes and colour changes are damage. Know which one you are arguing about.
The garden or balcony
Routinely forgotten and routinely charged for.
Limescale
Reads as neglect rather than dirt, which is why it is judged harshly.

Evidence to take

  1. Photograph every room wide, then every fail point close, on the day you hand back keys
  2. Photograph the meter readings with the same timestamp
  3. Keep the cleaning invoice — an itemised one, naming the property
  4. Get the inventory clerk’s report in writing, and dispute in writing within the deadline
  5. Compare against the check-in inventory rather than against your memory
