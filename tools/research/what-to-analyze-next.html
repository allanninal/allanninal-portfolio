01 — The short verdict

Money, power bills, and one dated election

Two independent sweeps ran against your 25 published analyses. One asked what readers want (news cycle, search demand, what travels on Reddit and Hacker News). The other asked where supply is weakest (what actually ranks today for 70 real queries, and how bad it is).

They converged hard on one theme: the cost of being alive in the Philippines. Wages, minimum wage, electricity, rice, cost of living. Every one of those queries is currently owned by lead-generation blogs, paywalled aggregators, or congressional PDFs — and every one has a free, authoritative dataset sitting behind it.

Four findings reframe the whole backlog:

  1. Eight of your 25 projects run on Kaggle mirrors, not primary sources. That is a freshness and provenance risk, and it is also free inventory: re-mining PSA OpenStat directly gives you longer series and finer geography than the mirror had.
  2. Nothing in Philippine data content claims schema.org/Dataset markup. Across 70 tested queries, zero dataset results appeared. Publishing a downloadable CSV plus Dataset markup on every page is an almost uncontested advantage.
  3. The moat is collection, not analysis. Six of the ten best gaps are gaps precisely because they need repeated scraping — grid alerts, board-exam results, rate filings, fuel and power prices, heat index. Statista will not pay that cost and news sites do not retain it. Automate the scrapers before writing the essays.
  4. Four sources cited in your published work are dead or frozen. Confirmed by direct request, not inference. The worst is Yahoo Finance, whose Philippine equity feed has been frozen since June 2019 — see the dead-source table below.

1. Flood control: rank 4, or skip entirely?

The demand sweep ranked it fourth. The supply sweep tested the query and said skip — Rappler's map, PCIJ's investigations, Wikipedia and BetterGov's DPWH dataset already own page one, all free and interactive. Only worth doing if your OFW analysis covered deployment and destinations rather than money. The distinct angle is purely financial: real, CPI- and FX-adjusted remittance value per receiving household, which — unlike the record nominal headlines — has probably been falling. Visual Capitalist, Our World in Data and Macrotrends already own it. Publish it only as the international companion to the Philippine baby-bust piece, sharing one notebook. Never standalone.

It collides with your published connectivity analysis. Demote it to a satellite article under that piece. Worth knowing: Ookla publishes free quarterly open data as parquet tiles, which is far better than the Statista page currently ranking with 2018 numbers.

07 — Free data sources

Verified sources behind the backlog

Provenance of this section

Both catalog sweeps have now completed. Every claim below marked as verified was confirmed with a live HTTP request on 2026-09-04 — status code and payload inspected, not inferred. Where a sweep could not confirm something, this page says so rather than presenting a lead as a fact.

Four findings that change how you build pipelines

1. The entire PSA domain is behind a Cloudflare challenge

psa.gov.ph, openstat.psa.gov.ph and psada.psa.gov.ph all return 403 with a managed-challenge header. A browser user-agent is not enough; it needs JavaScript execution. The same wall sits in front of coa.gov.ph, ched.gov.ph, lto.gov.ph, doh.gov.ph, foi.gov.ph and officialgazette.gov.ph.

So no cron job, script, or CI runner can refresh your PSA-dependent projects. Three ways out: drive a headless browser that solves the challenge once and caches; download manually to a local cache; or pull the same data from the HDX, World Bank and UN mirrors, which are unblocked and scriptable. That last option is why HDX carries so much weight in this catalog.

The barrier is also the moat. Most people give up at a 403, so a browser-driven pipeline gets you data competitors cannot script.

2. data.gov.ph is no longer CKAN, and fails silently

It is now a single-page app. The documented /api/3/action/package_search endpoint returns the HTML shell with status 200 — so old code "succeeds" while receiving garbage. That is the worst possible failure mode. The real backend at api.data.gov.ph returns 401 without a key that the page derives client-side. The clean route is to copy the request header from your own browser's network tab, or ask DICT for documented access.

3. Yahoo Finance no longer carries Philippine equities

Verified on SMPH.PS, ALI.PS and BDO.PS: all three return exchange YHD, a null price, instrument type MUTUALFUND, and regularMarketTime: 1561759658 — 28 June 2019. The ^PSEI index returns an empty result set. The old stooq bulk-CSV fallback is now behind a JavaScript anti-bot challenge.

The most promising replacement is PSE Edge (edge.pse.com.ph), whose disclosure endpoint returns 415 to a GET — meaning it is a JSON POST API worth an afternoon of work. It is also the authoritative source.

4. Philippine government works are not copyrighted — with one catch

Section 176 of the IP Code: "No copyright shall subsist in any work of the Government of the Philippines." No CC licence is granted because none is needed. The catch is that prior agency approval is contemplated for exploitation for profit. A portfolio or non-commercial analysis does not engage that clause, and charts you derive yourself are further removed still. Section 175 is unconditional for any official text of a legislative, administrative or legal nature — which makes statutes, the GAA legal text and COA report text the safest material available.

Practical rule: attribute the agency and publish. The real constraints in your stack are elsewhere — ODbL share-alike on everything OpenStreetMap-derived, and non-commercial clauses on Ookla open data, WHO GHO, and IMDb datasets. The Ookla NC clause matters the day the site carries ads.

Two access findings worth remembering

The official flood-control tracker has no export, and an FOI request for CSV was formally denied. A usable dataset exists only because a third party rebuilt it from DPWH contracts, bid bulletins and the DIME monitoring system. When a portal refuses machine-readable access, someone has usually already rebuilt it — check GitHub before assuming the data is unavailable.

Government PDFs are the most reliable moat you have. They cannot win featured snippets, cannot be sorted, are not mobile-readable, and are often years stale. Fourteen of the queries tested are owned by a PDF. Extraction is safe and the resulting HTML table wins on both relevance and engagement.

09 — Who else covers PH data

The competitive field, and the holes in it

Knowing who owns what tells you where not to spend effort. Four incumbents matter, and each leaves a specific hole.

  • PSA has the data and loses the search results. Press-release prose and raw cubes, almost no charts, no cross-dataset joins, rotting URLs.
  • Statista is the weakest strong incumbent — paywalled, often two to four years stale, no methodology, no download. It ranks on domain authority alone.
  • PhilAtlas covers every barangay, city and province — but population and administrative structure only, 2020 census, tables, no charts. No economic, health, education or electoral layer. This is the single biggest structural gap in Philippine data content.
  • BetterGov.ph is the fastest-rising competitor: budget, DPWH infrastructure 2004–2025, procurement, a dynasty graph of 86,200 memberships. But governance and spending only — no health, education, energy, labour, prices or agriculture.
  • Rappler and PCIJ do the best PH data journalism, but news-cycle and accountability driven. Almost no evergreen reference pages.
  • Vendor and law-firm blogs dominate every commercial query — salaries, BPO, annulment, electricity, condos — with unsourced recycled numbers. They are the lowest-quality page-one incumbents in the entire set, and the easiest to beat.

Under-covered by everyone

  1. Anything sub-national that isn't population
  2. Infrastructure service quality over time — grid alerts, water interruptions, outages
  3. Public-sector pay and workload as data
  4. Institutional-performance leaderboards — schools, jails, water districts, LGUs
  5. Money-flow-to-place joins — transfers per capita, collection vs regional GDP
  6. Culture and consumption as data — streaming, box office, PBA, esports, gambling
  7. Long-run agricultural decline
  8. Methodology explainers — why two official series disagree

11 — Moonshots

Five that could actually travel

1. Every flood-control project, mapped against where floods actually happened

Join the rebuilt DPWH project coordinates to satellite-observed flood extents from Sentinel-1 SAR for the 2022–2026 monsoons. Each peso-value dot coloured by whether the area it was built to protect still flooded, plus a ranked table of the most expensive projects in still-flooded barangays.

Why it spreads: it turns an abstract ₱545B scandal into something a reader can check for their own street. Personal-relevance lookup is the most shared dataviz mechanic there is, and it is falsifiable and source-open — which is what gets a data story cited rather than ignored.

2. Two curves of AI: tokens 84% cheaper, GPUs 40% more expensive

One chart, two lines, 2023 to 2026. Frontier token prices collapsing against H100 contract rental rising, annotated with the arbitrage that explains it.

Why it spreads: a paradox with a resolution is the exact shape of a top Hacker News thread. Every AI engineer has felt both curves and nobody has drawn them together. Publish the tracker and every future price cut is a free repost.

3. What one hour of minimum wage buys, 1989 to 2026

Deflate every regional wage order by CPI, then express it in goods: kilos of rice, kilowatt-hours of Meralco electricity, jeepney rides, one now-VAT-inclusive streaming subscription.

Why it spreads: it converts an abstract political fight into units everyone already prices in their head. Zero-competition dataset, politically legible to both sides, and it reposts on every future wage order forever.

4. The country that stopped having children

The 2025 NDHS at province resolution, small-multipled — 4.1 in 1993 down to 1.7, with CALABARZON at 1.3 — plus an animated cohort pyramid running to 2100.

Why it spreads: fertility collapse is 2026's most-shared data genre, but every viral version is Korea, Japan or Italy. The Philippines was the counterexample everyone reached for. Retiring that assumption with the government's own brand-new survey is a global-tier story from a Philippine desk.

5. Six weeks, one repo: scraping every PH job posting to find where AI ate the junior roles

Daily scrape of the major PH job boards, classified by seniority and AI mention, charted against IBPAP's employment projections and its quietly lowered 2028 ceiling.

Why it spreads: it hits three audiences at once, and the scraper is itself the tutorial — so the method post and the findings post are two pieces of content. Self-collected data is what Reddit and Hacker News reward, and because Indeed and Lightcast publish no downloadable microdata, nobody can scoop you with an API call.

13 — What this research still owes you

What is verified, and what is still a lead

All four sweeps have now completed. The two catalog sweeps initially died on rate limits and were resumed after the account limit reset; they finished with direct HTTP verification rather than search, which is why the source claims in this report carry status codes behind them.

What is confirmed: every source in section 7 marked as verified was hit live on 2026-09-04. The four dead sources in your published work were confirmed twice — once by the sweep, once by me independently before I touched any file.

What is still unverified, stated plainly so you do not build on a lead by mistake:

  • Behind the Cloudflare wall, nothing could be checked. 2024 POPCEN results, the latest FIES round, 2023 poverty statistics, current LFS/SOF rounds, the current DOH surveillance week — all sit on PSA and DOH domains that return 403 to any script. These are the most likely refresh wins for your existing projects, and they need a browser. Check them manually.
  • Kaggle dataset pages are JavaScript-rendered, so per-dataset last-updated and licence could not be read. The replacement primaries are right regardless, but the staleness of each specific mirror is unconfirmed.
  • PRC board-exam results: prc.gov.ph returns 200 and is not blocked, but the results index path was not found. Worth ten minutes in a browser — it unlocks the highest-frequency compounding asset in the backlog.
  • COA audit reports: a download URL pattern was worked out, then the domain 403'd. Needs browser-driven enumeration.
  • Not reached at all: SEC company registry, LTFRB, DILG SGLG detail, Open Contracting PH, BFAR NSAP, Indonesia BPS, Vietnam GSO, Thailand NSO, ADB Data Library. Also unverified in the global sweep: NOAA GHCN-Daily, EM-DAT direct, IHME GBD (non-commercial terms), Semantic Scholar, HTTP Archive.

Three sources worth building on first, all verified, all keyless:

  • DA Bantay Presyo daily price PDFs — live to today, clean URL pattern, archive back to at least 2023. Daily rice, vegetable and fish prices per Metro Manila market. The scrape is the contribution: this multi-year daily food-price panel does not exist in machine-readable form anywhere. Best originality-per-hour in the catalog.
  • Project CCHAIN — barangay-level health, climate and socioeconomic data for 12 cities, 2003–2022, CC BY, already tidy CSVs. The highest-ceiling unused Philippine dataset found, and it makes dengue-versus-rainfall analysis possible at a resolution nobody has published.
  • NOAA IBTrACS — every western-Pacific cyclone track since 1884, three-hourly, with wind and pressure. Combined with geoBoundaries and WorldPop, it gives population-weighted typhoon exposure by province. Every input is keyless and openly licensed.

Two threads stayed open. The CHED JSON API could not be tested because ched.gov.ph sits behind the same Cloudflare wall. One licence question did get closed: Global Energy Monitor has no Creative Commons licence at all. Its pages carry only a copyright notice and a requested citation format, and the terms-of-use URLs 404. Usable with citation for derived charts under normal editorial practice — but do not call it openly licensed and do not redistribute the raw file.

Method. Four subagents, two completed. 70 live search queries testing what currently ranks, plus 32 demand-side searches with sources cited inline.
