Key Takeaways

Engel’s law says the share of income spent on food falls as income rises. It holds here across all ten deciles without a single break — from 59.62% down to 21.36%.

  • Median household income was P164,080 against a mean of P247,556 — the mean is 1.51 times the median, which is what a long right tail does to an average.
  • The richest tenth took 34.32% of all income in the sample against 2.16% for the poorest tenth. The top 1% alone took 8.14%.
  • Agricultural households had a median income of P105,157 against P182,696 for the rest — a factor of 1.74.
  • These are 41,544 unweighted household records. FIES ships sampling weights so results can be grossed up to the population; this public extract does not include them, so every figure describes the sample and not the country.

Engel's Law, Without Exception

The oldest empirical regularity in economics: as income rises, the share spent on food falls. Across all ten deciles here it falls monotonically — a check asserts it, because a break would mean the decile assignment or the ratio is wrong.

What This Page Does Not Cover

The microdata is rich, and this page uses a fraction of its sixty columns. Three limits are worth stating rather than leaving to be discovered.

The sample is unweighted

FIES ships sampling weights so results can be grossed up to the population. This public extract does not include them, so every figure here describes the 41,544 sampled households and not the Philippines. National totals are not computed and would be wrong if they were.

It is 2015

A decade old. Nominal peso figures from 2015 are not comparable with today's, and nothing here is inflation-adjusted. Later FIES rounds exist and sit behind PSA's managed challenge.

Housing and assets are in the file, not on the page

Type of roof, walls, building, toilet facility and water source are all columns here. They deserve their own treatment against the national JMP series rather than a chart each, and are left for that rather than added thinly.

Method

One fetcher, seven CSVs, from the public mirror the page already cited.

PSA is unreachable; the mirror is not

psa.gov.ph sits behind a managed challenge that scripts do not pass. The public FIES extract is mirrored on Kaggle and served without authentication. The previous version of this page linked to that mirror and never opened it.

The microdata is not committed

22 MB, redistributable but not ours, and a checked-in copy would go stale against the mirror without anyone noticing. The fetcher downloads, aggregates and writes only the summaries.

Medians, not means

Income is strongly right-skewed -- the mean is 1.51 times the median and the largest single household reported P11,815,988. Medians are used throughout and the mean appears only where the gap is the point.

Subtotals are excluded by name

The file carries Total Food Expenditure alongside its components. Every spending category is named explicitly so a subtotal cannot be summed with the parts it contains.

Engel's law is asserted

A check fails if the food share stops falling monotonically across deciles. It holds essentially without exception in household budget data, so a break would indicate a fault here rather than a finding.

Four figures that were already right

41,544 households, P247,556 mean, P164,080 median and a 0.444 Gini were all on the previous version of this page and all check out against the microdata. The charts did not.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Engel's law holds across all ten deciles without a break: food takes 59.62% of income at the bottom and 21.36% at the top, a 38.26-point spread.
  • Median household income was P164,080 against a mean of P247,556 — 1.51 times higher, the signature of a long right tail.
  • The richest tenth held 34.32% of sample income against 2.16% for the poorest, giving a Gini of 0.4438 computed from these records.
  • Agricultural households earned P105,157 at the median against P182,696 for everyone else, and NCR earned 2.8 times ARMM.
  • All of it describes 41,544 unweighted sampled households in 2015, not the country and not today.
