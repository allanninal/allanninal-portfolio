Half The Journals Labelled Free-To-Publish Charge A Fee

OpenAlex sorts every paper into six open-access states. One, “diamond”, means the journal is fully open and charges the author nothing — 1,577,668 works in 2023. DOAJ asks journals that question directly. Of 167 journals carrying the label in this audit, 79 charge a fee, and the disagreement is 2.4 times more likely when the price is not in dollars or euros.

Key Takeaways

Of the 167 journals OpenAlex labels diamond in this audit — fully open and free for the author — 79 charge an author fee according to DOAJ, which asks them directly. That is 47.31%.

  • The error is not random. 21.14% of fee-charging journals priced in a hard currency carry the label, against 50.6% of those priced in anything else — 2.4 times more likely. OpenAlex has no dollar price on file for those journals and a missing price is being read as no price.
  • The other arm of the audit is reassuring and belongs here too: of 90 journals DOAJ says charge nothing, 88 are labelled diamond. The classification over-includes; it does not miss genuinely free journals.
  • That makes the country map of diamond open access partly a map of which currencies have been converted. Indonesia leads at 63.61% against Germany’s 5.03% — 12.6 times — and 21 of the 46 fee-charging rupiah-priced journals here carry the label.
  • The six states are genuinely different things, and the data says so: 58.18% of gold works and 41.47% of hybrid works carry a recorded charge, against 0.03% of diamond and 0.0% of closed. Only two of the six move money.
  • Asked directly rather than inferred, DOAJ's own answer is the cleaner one: 14,670 of 23,402 indexed journals — 62.69% — charge no author fee at all.

Six States, Not Two

“62.33% of research is open access” sums five different arrangements. Three of them can cost an author money, one is revocable, and one means only that a manuscript sits in a repository while the published version stays behind a paywall.

The Error Follows The Currency

OpenAlex records an article charge in US dollars. For a journal that prices in rupiah, rial or hryvnia there is often no dollar figure on file — and a missing price is being read as no price.

What Would Settle It

This page shows the label is unreliable. It does not show what the true country ranking is, and three specific things would.

A dollar price for every journal

The whole mechanism is a null in one field. If OpenAlex carried a converted charge for the journals that price in rupiah and rial, the classification would be right about them and the country map could be read directly.

The audit at full size

348 journals is enough to establish the rate and its currency dependence. It is not enough to correct sixty countries one by one. Doing that means auditing every journal each country publishes in, which is a much larger job than a daily API allowance permits.

Asking DOAJ first

DOAJ already answers the question directly for 23,402 journals, 62.69% of which charge nothing. Building the country map from that answer rather than from an inferred label would avoid the problem entirely, at the cost of covering only DOAJ-indexed journals.

What These Numbers Are Not

Four limits, and the first is the one that decides how far the headline travels.

A sample, and a budget-limited one

The audit covers 348 journals: 258 that DOAJ records as charging and 90 as not. OpenAlex allows about a thousand requests a day without an account and each journal costs two, so the sample stopped where the allowance did rather than at a planned size. 3 sampled journals were not in OpenAlex at all.

DOAJ is not infallible either

It records what a journal told it, and journals change their fees. Where the two sources disagree this page assumes DOAJ is right because it asked; that assumption is doing real work and is worth stating plainly.

A journal-level label on work-level counts

The state is assigned per work but decided by the journal, so a journal labelled wrongly mislabels everything it published. That is why a modest share of journals can move a national percentage a long way.

Nothing here is about quality

Charging an author fee is not evidence of a bad journal and charging none is not evidence of a good one. The only claim on this page is that a field which says a journal charges nothing frequently describes a journal that charges something.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Of 167 journals labelled diamond — free to read and free to publish — 79 charge an author fee: 47.31%.
  • The error tracks currency: 21.14% of fee-charging journals priced in a hard currency carry the label against 50.6% of the rest, 2.4 times as likely.
  • The label over-includes rather than misses: of 90 genuinely fee-free journals, 88 are labelled correctly.
  • So the country ranking is partly a currency artefact. Indonesia leads at 63.61% against Germany’s 5.03%, and 21 of 46 fee-charging rupiah-priced journals here carry the label.
  • Only two of the six states move money: 58.18% of gold and 41.47% of hybrid works carry a recorded charge, against 0.03% of diamond.
  • Asked directly, DOAJ says 14,670 of 23,402 journals — 62.69% — charge no author fee.

Sources & Citations

Every figure on this page traces to one of these, through a CSV in data/global-openaccess/. Each is checked against its source query on every build.

OpenAlexPrimary

Every indexed scholarly work 2014-2024 grouped by open-access status, by year and by author country; journal records with a converted article processing charge in US dollars — CC0, no key, polite pool with a mailto. The oa_status field is the subject of this page rather than its evidence: a work is labelled diamond when its journal is fully open and no author fee is recorded, and 'no fee recorded' is not the same claim as 'no fee'.

DOAJPrimary

23,402 indexed open-access journals with a direct yes/no answer on whether the journal charges an author fee, plus the amount and the currency — CC BY-SA, no key. DOAJ asks the journal and records what it says, including the currency. It is the closest thing to a direct answer and it is what the audit on this page compares against.

World Bank World Development IndicatorsPrimary

Income group, region and GNI per capita (NY.GNP.PCAP.CD) for each country — No key. Used only to describe the countries, never to compute an open-access figure.

OpenAlex open-access status documentation

The published definition of the six oa_status values — Cited so the gap between the documented definition and the observed behaviour is checkable rather than asserted.
