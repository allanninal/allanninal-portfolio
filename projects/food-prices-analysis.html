Twenty-Six Years Of Food Prices, And Only Two Of Them

214,215 WFP price observations across 62 foods and 97 markets. The file runs from 2000, but only 2 commodities run the whole way — so every rate here is scoped to its own span, and the two that share all 25 years disagree by a factor of 2.23.

Key Takeaways

Pork rose 299.9% between 2000 and 2025. Rice, over the identical 25 years, rose 134.2% — a factor of 2.23. They are the only two commodities in the file that span the whole record, which is why they are the only two compared this way.

  • The dataset looks 26 years deep and mostly is not. 3 commodities start in January 2000, 11 in January 2008, and 46 — 74.2% of them — only in May 2020.
  • Red onions went from 106.25 pesos a kilo in June 2022 to 487.5 in 2023-01, a factor of 4.59. One market reached 617.5. That month onions cost 1.56 times as much per kilo as pork.
  • The gap between a food's rise in pesos and its rise in dollars is the peso's own decline: 106.9 points for pork. 8 commodities rose in pesos while falling in dollars.
  • Vegetables and fruits rose fastest as a group, a median 46.9%, against 18.8% for cereals and tubers — the category rice is in.
  • Metro Manila is not where rice is dearest. It ranks 5th cheapest of 17 regions at 40.66 a kilo, against 44.56 in Autonomous region in Muslim Mindanao.

What This Page Does Not Claim

Four limits, three of them asserted by checks so they cannot quietly stop being true.

There is no basket index

WFP prices carry no household consumption weights, so there is no way to say what a typical family's food bill did. Averaging 62 commodities would weight garlic like rice. Every figure here is about a named commodity, and a check asserts the weights column stays at zero.

Quality is assumed constant

A kilo of tomatoes in 2008 and a kilo in 2025 are treated as the same thing. They may not be. This is an assumption, not a measurement, and it is the standard one — but it means a price rise and a quality rise are indistinguishable here.

2026 is excluded from every rate

The file reaches June 2026. Ending a change figure there would compare six months against twelve and report part of the seasonal cycle as inflation. A check fails if any commodity's last year is later than 2025.

Nothing here is inflation-adjusted

These are nominal pesos. General consumer inflation over the same period accounts for part of every rise on this page, and separating food inflation from it needs a CPI series this project does not carry. The dollar column in section 3 is a different adjustment — for the exchange rate, not for prices at home.

Method

One derivation script over a file that was already in the repo.

The source was already here

wfp_food_prices_phl.csv, 31 MB and 234,015 rows, has been in data/ph-food-prices since the rice page was built. The previous version of this page published twenty-eight figures without opening it.

Retail, per kilogramme, actual only

5,578 WFP aggregate rows are excluded because they are computed from the actual rows and counting both double-counts. 7,850 non-kilogramme rows are excluded because a per-unit price and a per-kilo price are different measurements. What remains is 214,215 observations.

Medians, not means

A national price for a month is the median across markets, and an annual price is the median across that year's monthly observations. Means would let one market's outlier move a national figure, which in the onion months it would have done considerably.

Every rate is scoped to its own span

No commodity is measured against a fixed 2000 baseline it does not have. Each row carries its own first and last year and the change between them, and a check verifies the change and the compound rate agree with those two endpoint prices.

The currency check runs on direction

The peso fell against the dollar across this whole period, so every commodity's rise in pesos must exceed its rise in dollars. A check fails on any crossing, because that would mean the two currency columns had been swapped — which is not the kind of error that looks wrong on a chart.

This page and the rice page share a directory

Both read the same 31 MB source, so they share data/ph-food-prices rather than keeping two copies. Fact keys beginning food. belong here; keys beginning rice. belong to the rice page.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Pork rose 299.9% and rice 134.2% over the identical 25 years — a factor of 2.23, and they are the only two commodities that span the whole record.
  • 46 of the 62 commodities only start in May 2020, so the file's 26-year range describes the file rather than most of what is in it.
  • Onions peaked at 487.5 pesos a kilo in 2023-01, up 4.59× from June 2022, with one market at 617.5 — 1.56 times the price of pork.
  • The peso-dollar gap on pork is 106.9 points, and 8 commodities rose in pesos while falling in dollars.
  • Vegetables and fruits rose a median 46.9% against 18.8% for cereals and tubers.
  • Rice is dearest in Autonomous region in Muslim Mindanao at 44.56 and cheapest in Region II at 35.22; Metro Manila is 5th cheapest of 17.
