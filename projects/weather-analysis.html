How Much Warmer? That Depends Which Model You Ask

1,117,692 daily observations across 9 Philippine grid cells, 1940 to 2024. Two reanalyses of the same atmosphere put Manila's warming at 0.072 and 0.122 °C a decade — so this page reports ranges. Both agree 8 of the 9 cities set their hottest day on record since 2020.

Key Takeaways

Two reanalyses of the same atmosphere disagree by 1.09 °C on Manila's mean temperature and by a factor of 1.9 on how fast it is warming. That is not a rounding detail, so no single absolute temperature appears on this page and every trend is a range.

  • Across the 6 cities checked both ways over the 1950–2024 period they share, warming runs from 0.072 to 0.131 °C a decade on ERA5-Land and 0.122 to 0.25 on ERA5. The two agree on direction in 6 of 6 cities — all of them.
  • The firmest finding is a count, not a slope. 8 of the 9 cities set their hottest day on record in 2020 or later; only Baguio's stands, from 1966. And 8 of 9 had their warmest complete decade in the 2010s.
  • Days above 35 °C in Laoag went from 0.3 a year in the 1950s to 30.99 in the 2020s; Manila from 3.0 to 20.39. A threshold count depends on absolute temperature, though, and the models are more than a degree apart — so the direction is solid and the magnitude is not.
  • Manila's coolest complete decade was the 1970s at 26.43 °C and its warmest the 2010s at 26.9. The 2020s hold only 5 years and are labelled partial everywhere they appear.
  • This is reanalysis: a physical model constrained by whatever was measured, on a grid of roughly 25 km. Not a thermometer record, and not fine enough to see a city apart from the land around it.

The Same Atmosphere, Reconstructed Twice

ERA5 and ERA5-Land are both ECMWF reanalyses of the same period, the second at higher resolution over land. Where they disagree is the honest measure of how well any of this is known, and they disagree more than most charts of warming would suggest.

What Reanalysis Is Not

This page is built on a model, and that changes what its numbers mean.

It is not a thermometer record

ERA5 is a physical model of the atmosphere run backwards, constrained by whatever observations existed at the time. Its 1940s values rest on far fewer measurements than its 2020s ones, which is part of why the two reanalyses disagree more about early decades than recent ones. PAGASA holds the Philippine station record and is not reachable from a script here.

A city is a grid cell

Roughly 25 km for ERA5 and 10 km for ERA5-Land. That cannot separate Manila from the land around it, so nothing here measures urban heat island effects, and "Manila" means a cell centred near it rather than the city.

No storms

Daily rainfall totals do not identify a typhoon, and wind is not in this dataset at all. The typhoon page uses IBTrACS track data for that, which is a different archive entirely.

The trend is a range and stays one

Reporting the midpoint of two models that differ by a factor of 1.9 would be a false precision. Where this page gives a single trend it names the model it came from, and a check fails if the two ever converge to within 0.02 °C a decade, because then this whole framing should be revisited.

Method

One fetcher, nine cities, eight CSVs, and a cache.

Both models are asked explicitly

The API's default silently resolves to one of them. A page that quotes "the" temperature without saying which model produced it is quoting a coin flip, so era5 and era5_land are named in the request and carried through as separate rows.

Trends use only the shared years

ERA5 begins in 1940 and ERA5-Land in 1950. Comparing a 1940-2024 slope against a 1950-2024 one is partly a comparison of periods, and the early decades are exactly the ones whose inclusion changes the answer. Each model's own full-span trend is kept in a separate row labelled with its span.

A year needs 360 days to count

Partial years are excluded from every mean and trend. A missing January would otherwise drag a mean down and read as a cold year, and a check asserts no annual row falls below the threshold.

Partial decades are labelled, not hidden

The 2020s hold 5 years. They appear on the charts because leaving them off would hide the warmest data in the record, and they carry their year count because plotting five years beside ten as though both were decades would be worse.

The cross-check runs on six cities, not nine

Open-Meteo's free tier counts an 85-year request as many calls and returns 429 well before 27 of them finish. Six cities from Luzon to Mindanao establish the disagreement as well as nine would; all nine still get the full daily series. The cities chosen are named in the fetcher rather than left implicit.

Responses are cached on disk

About 3 MB of JSON per city per model. Cached so that changing an aggregate does not re-fetch 85 years from a free API eighteen times over, and not committed.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Two reanalyses of the same atmosphere sit 1.09 °C apart on Manila's mean and a factor of 1.9 apart on its warming rate, so every trend here is a range and no single absolute temperature is quoted.
  • Warming runs 0.072–0.131 °C a decade on ERA5-Land and 0.122–0.25 on ERA5, and both agree on direction in all 6 cities checked.
  • 8 of 9 cities set their hottest day on record since 2020 — the exception is Baguio, from 1966 — and 8 of 9 had their warmest complete decade in the 2010s.
  • Days above 35 °C rose from 0.3 a year to 30.99 in Laoag and from 3.0 to 20.39 in Manila.
  • Rainfall varies far more than temperature: 3,171 mm a year in Baguio against 1,543 in Manila, and Laoag's wettest month is 28 times its driest.
  • All of it is reanalysis on a ~25 km grid, not station data. It cannot see a city apart from the land around it.

Sources & Citations

Every figure on this page traces to one of these, through a CSV in data/ph-weather/. Each is checked against its source query on every build.

Open-Meteo historical weather archive (ECMWF ERA5)Primary

Daily maximum, minimum and mean temperature and precipitation for nine points across the Philippines, 31,047 days each from 1 January 1940 to 31 December 2024 — Reanalysis, not observation: a physical model constrained by whatever measurements existed, on a grid of roughly 25 km. A city here is a grid cell, not a weather station.

ECMWF ERA5-LandPrimary

The same variables from the higher-resolution land-surface reanalysis, used as an independent check on ERA5 — Fetched through Open-Meteo. It disagrees with ERA5 by 1.09 °C on Manila's mean temperature and by roughly a factor of two on the warming rate, which is why this page reports a range rather than a number.

PAGASA climatological normalsSecondary

The Philippine authority for station observations and climate normals — Cited because it is what should be used and is not. pagasa.dost.gov.ph is not reachable from a script here, and station records are a different measurement from reanalysis.
