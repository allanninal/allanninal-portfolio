Philippine Rice Price Analysis

Twenty-six years of prices, from the farm gate to the shelf

Two sources joined: 25,624 rice observations from the World Food Programme via HDX, which is the only open series carrying farm gate and wholesale prices as well as retail, and 7,586 daily NCR retail prices scraped from the Department of Agriculture's Bantay Presyo PDFs. Together they cover January 2000 to September 2026.

Key Takeaways

The 2019 Rice Tariffication Law cut the wholesale price of rice by 20% in two years. Retail fell 10%. The gap between what traders pay and what shoppers pay doubled, and it has not gone back.

  • Retail rice went from P18.74/kg in 2000 to P54.37/kg in 2026 — roughly 2.9 times, before adjusting for inflation.
  • From 2000 to 2018 the wholesale-to-retail spread sat between 5.0% and 14.4%, averaging 10.1%. In 2019 it jumped to 16.0%, then 21.0% in 2020.
  • The 2008 world food crisis reset the level permanently: retail went from P24.02 in 2007 to P31.77 in 2008 and never returned.
  • 2024 was the most expensive year in the series at P55.58/kg, easing to P49.28 in 2025.
  • Imported and local rice now cost within about a peso of each other at the same grade — local carried a clear premium as recently as 2021.

The finding

The Tariff Law Cut Wholesale. Retail Barely Moved.

The Rice Tariffication Law took effect in March 2019, replacing import quotas with a tariff and letting private traders import freely. Wholesale prices fell as intended. The shelf price did not follow.

Wholesale-to-Retail Spread, 2000-2020

One grade held constant (regular milled), so a changing basket cannot explain the move

Nineteen years of stability
10.1%

From 2000 to 2018 the spread never left the 5.0-14.4% band. Whatever else changed over those years — and rice went up 2.2 times — the share taken between the warehouse and the shelf did not.

Then it doubled
21.0%

Wholesale fell from P38.47 to P30.95 across 2019 and 2020, a 20% drop. Retail fell from P41.43 to P37.46, about 10%. Roughly half the saving stopped somewhere between the two.

Imported vs local

The Import Premium Has Nearly Closed

Premium-grade rice in Metro Manila wet markets, imported against local, from the daily Bantay Presyo reports. Same grade, same day, same markets.

Premium Rice: Imported vs Local

Monthly mean of daily prevailing prices, PHP/kg. The line breaks where the source PDFs are unparseable — it is not drawn through the gap.

Within a peso
P1.11

In September 2026 premium rice cost P54.23/kg local against P53.12 imported — a gap of about one peso. At regular-milled grade local is now slightly cheaper than imported.

What this does not show

A narrowing gap is not automatically good news for farmers. It is consistent with local prices being pulled down toward import parity, which is what liberalisation was designed to do, and with farm gate incomes falling alongside.

Methodology

How This Was Built

Two sources, one of them scraped out of PDFs. The coverage is partial and the gaps are recorded rather than smoothed over.

📊

WFP via HDX (2000-2026)

wfp_food_prices_phl.csv, CC BY-IGO, refreshed 2026-08-30. 25,624 rice observations: 22,076 retail, 2,886 wholesale, 662 farm gate. The only open Philippine series carrying all three levels of the chain.

🌾

DA Bantay Presyo (2019-2026)

The Department of Agriculture publishes prevailing retail prices daily, as PDFs only. da.gov.ph/price-monitoring lists 3,230 files on one page. 7,586 price rows across 836 trading days were extracted with pdftotext -layout and column parsing.

⚠️

Coverage is 35%, and that is stated

841 of 2,367 dated PDFs parsed. 2022 and 2023 yield nothing: those years use a graphic layout with prices in boxes rather than table rows. Per-file status is recorded in ph_rice_prices_coverage.csv, not just in a log.

🔍

One grade held constant

The spread analysis uses regular milled rice only. The mix of grades reported changes over the years, so an all-rice average moves when the basket moves. Holding one grade fixed removes that.

🧪

Checks that run before publication

Vocabulary closure on variety names, coverage continuity by year, and a per-series price envelope — per commodity, not global, because Basmati legitimately trades near P200 and a global band flags 173 good rows. See checks.sql.

📌

Two source quirks, left as published

The DA's own PDF for 27 May 2026 prints "sGlutinous Rice"; the one for 14 November 2025 prints P60.00 for Basmati against a usual P200. Both were checked against the source and are faithful. The typo is corrected through a documented map; the price stands.
