Who Gets The Money For A Kilo Of Rice

Retail rice has nearly tripled since 2000. In the years where the whole chain can be seen, the farmer received under half of what the shopper paid — and a kilo costs 30.5% more in one region than another in the same year.

Key Takeaways

A national average rice price hides two things that matter more than the average: how little of it reaches the farmer, and how much it varies by where you shop.

  • In 2013, farmgate was 15.36 and retail 34.45 per kilo — the farmer got 44.6%, and across the 5 years where all three prices exist the share never left 43.2–47.9%.
  • In 2025, rice averaged 56.42 per kilo in Region VII and 43.24 in Region II — a gap of 13.18.
  • Variety matters as much: Rice (special) at 61.42 against Rice (regular, milled) at 47.8. A "rice price" that does not say which rice is not a price.
  • The daily series behind the recent years is built from government PDFs, of which 841 of 2367 parsed — 36%. That is published rather than hidden.

What This Page Does Not Cover

Rice prices, from two sources, at national and regional level. The obvious next questions are not answered here.

Why the margin is what it is

Milling, drying, transport, credit and trader margins all sit between farmgate and shelf. None are in these CSVs, so the page reports the size of the gap and says nothing about who takes which part of it.

Recent farmgate prices

The chain table covers 5 years only, ending in 2013. Farmgate prices are collected by PSA and its portal is not reachable by script, so the farmer's share cannot be brought up to date.

Everything that is not rice

The WFP file holds vegetables, meat, fish and pulses across the same markets — over 200,000 observations. This page is only rice; the rest is unexplored rather than excluded on principle.

Method

Two sources, one derive step, seven CSVs.

Two price series, kept apart

WFP market prices run 2000-2026 and DA Bantay Presyo supplies the daily NCR series from 2018. They are different collections with different coverage and are never spliced into one line.

Only actual prices

The WFP file carries aggregated and forecast rows alongside observations. The rice views filter to priceflag 'actual' and pricetype 'retail'; without that the series mixes measurements with estimates.

Coverage is published

841 of 2367 source PDFs parsed — 36%. The unparsed ones are mostly a layout generation the parser does not handle, and they are listed per document with a status rather than left in a log.

A regex that burned an hour

The Bantay Presyo parser began as one pattern with nested quantifiers and spent 65 minutes of CPU backtracking on wide per-market rows. It now splits fixed-layout rows on runs of two or more spaces, which cannot blow up.

Varieties are not averaged

Five rice commodities are reported separately. Collapsing them into one national price makes the series move when the reporting mix changes.

Verification

Every figure on this page is bound to a query in facts.sql and re-checked on each build; checks.sql adds vocabulary closure and per-series price envelopes.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Retail rice is 2.9 times its 2000 level in nominal pesos, from P18.74 to P54.37 per kilo.
  • The farmer's share of that never reaches half: 43.2 to 47.9% across every year where farmgate, wholesale and retail were all collected.
  • Region matters: P56.42 per kilo in Region VII against P43.24 in Region II in the same year.
  • Variety matters more: P61.42 for Rice (special) against P47.8 for Rice (regular, milled). Any single "price of rice" figure is choosing one of 5 without saying so.
  • The import premium has nearly closed since the 2019 tariff law, to P1.11 per kilo.
