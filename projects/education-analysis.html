A Whole New Tier Of School, Built In Four Years

DepEd public-school counts for 11 academic years and all 17 regions. Senior high school did not exist until SY 2016-2017; by SY 2020-2021 it held 1,987,180 pupils. Over the same period teachers grew 4.97 times as fast as enrollment.

Key Takeaways

Enrollment rose 14.61% across the eleven years. Teacher numbers rose 72.66% — 369,009 more teachers, growing 4.97 times as fast — and the pupil-teacher ratio fell from 38.93 to 25.84.

  • Senior high school appears in SY 2016-2017 with 731,981 pupils and reaches 1,987,180 by SY 2020-2021, a factor of 2.71. It is now 8.77% of all public basic-education enrollment.
  • It is also the one tier where crowding got worse. Senior high opened at 19.9 pupils per teacher, hit 29.57 the following year as enrollment doubled, and was still 26.83 in the last year — while elementary fell from 39.38 to 26.51.
  • The biggest senior-high track is 37.84% TVL — Technical-Vocational-Livelihood — against STEM's 9.02%. That is 4.2 times as many pupils, and it puts STEM 4th of 8.
  • None of that says anything about learning, and the national figures are less comfortable: 8.55% of a primary cohort does not complete primary, and 1,616,165 primary-age children were out of school in 2024.
  • These are public schools only. Roughly a tenth of Philippine basic education is private and is absent from every DepEd figure here.

What This Page Does Not Claim

Four limits, all recorded in the coverage file and asserted by checks.

Public schools only

DepEd counts the schools it runs. Roughly a tenth of Philippine basic education is private, and it is absent from every DepEd figure here — including the pupil-teacher ratios, which would look different if private schools were in them.

Enrollment is not learning

There are no test scores in this file, no completion rates and no grades. It records who is on a roll. The national figures in section 6 are the nearest this project gets to an outcome, and they are enrolment and completion rather than attainment.

Pupils per teacher is not class size

A teacher may take several classes and a class several teachers, and the file carries neither class nor school counts. The ratio is a staffing measure. It is a real one — it is what changed most over the decade — but it is not the number of children in a room.

Nobody is followed between years

Each row is one region in one year, so no pupil cohort can be tracked and no dropout or repetition rate can be computed from this. The rise in junior-high enrollment alongside the fall in elementary is consistent with a cohort moving up, but this data cannot confirm that it is the same children.

Method

One fetcher over a 187-row file, plus the World Bank for what the file cannot say.

Senior-high columns are matched by prefix

The source spells one of them Enrollees_G122_SPORTs — a typo for G12. Matching by prefix counts it; a hand-kept list would have dropped 3,809 pupils and the totals would still have looked plausible. A check asserts the three levels sum to the national total, which is what would catch it next time.

Each level's ratio uses only its own staff

Elementary enrollment against elementary teachers, and so on. A blended figure hides that senior high moved the opposite way from the other two, which is the most interesting thing in the file.

A reporting break is detected, not hardcoded

Indonesia's education spending falls from 3.58% of GDP to 1.21% in one year and stays down, because it devolves most spending to its regions and changed what it reports to UIS. Any country whose series halves and stays down is flagged automatically, with the break year recorded, rather than a name being written into the script.

Two figures the page pairs share a year on purpose

Net secondary enrolment stops at 2015 and gross runs to 2024. Taking each at its own latest year would put 65.56% and 85.29% beside a gap of 16.45 points — three numbers that do not add up. A check fails if the year the page pairs them on ever loses one of them.

Regions are shortened for display only

The source spells one “BARMM - Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao”. Cards use the acronym; the CSVs keep the full string, so a figure still traces to a row that names the region in full.

The counterweight comes through the World Bank

UIS completion, enrolment, out-of-school and spending series, fetched from the WDI API because it is reachable and returns a clean error on a wrong indicator code rather than an empty success.

Key Findings & Summary

  • Enrollment rose 14.61% while teachers rose 72.66% — 369,009 more posts, growing 4.97 times as fast — taking pupils per teacher from 38.93 to 25.84.
  • Senior high school went from nothing to 1,987,180 pupils in four years, now 8.77% of public basic education.
  • It is the only tier that got more crowded: 19.9 pupils per teacher at launch, 29.57 a year later, 26.83 at the end — against elementary's 26.51.
  • TVL is 37.84% of senior high against STEM's 9.02% — 4.2 times as many pupils, putting STEM 4th of 8.
  • BARMM runs 32.14 pupils per teacher against CAR's 20.46.
  • None of it measures learning. Nationally 8.55% of a primary cohort does not complete primary and 1,616,165 primary-age children were out of school in 2024.
