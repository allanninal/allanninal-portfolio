What happens to a permit nobody uses

Circumstances change, people move house, start cycling, or change their working pattern, and a permit issued two years ago on the basis of a situation that no longer exists is capacity sitting idle.

Key takeaways

  • Measure usage crudely; a rough count is enough and needs no surveillance.
  • Warn, explain, then reclaim. Never reclaim without a conversation.
  • Reasons that are good: illness, leave, a working pattern that changed temporarily.
  • Reclaimed permits are the largest source of capacity available.
  • An annual reapplication achieves much of this with less machinery.

Measuring usage proportionately

Just asking works

An annual message saying “you have a parking permit; do you still need it?” with two buttons reclaims a meaningful number with no measurement at all. People who have started cycling or moved closer are frequently happy to release it, and nobody had ever asked.

It is worth doing before building any usage tracking, because it is free, it is not surveillance, and it may solve most of the problem. If it does, the tracking is unnecessary.

If usage is tracked

Aggregate is enough: a count of days used per quarter per permit. There is no need to store which days, and storing which days creates a record of somebody’s attendance pattern that was collected for parking and will eventually be asked for by somebody else.

That restraint is worth building in deliberately: count and discard rather than store and count. It is barely more work and it removes an entire category of future question.

Warn, explain, reclaim

Reasons that are good

Long-term sickness, parental leave, a secondment, a temporary change in working pattern, a period of working from home for a reason. All of these are situations where somebody will need the permit again and taking it away is both unkind and pointless.

The review-next-quarter outcome handles them properly: the permit is retained, the situation is noted, and it is looked at again rather than either forgotten or removed.

Where capacity comes from

Restriping is worth mentioning because it occasionally produces real gains — a car park laid out for larger vehicles, or with awkward corners, can sometimes yield a few more bays — and it is a one-off with a modest cost.

Building new spaces is generally not available, is expensive where it is, and takes long enough that the working pattern will have changed before it opens.

The simpler alternative

Annual reapplication achieves much of what reclaim does with less machinery: everybody reapplies each year, the rule is applied afresh, and permits that are no longer needed simply are not applied for.

It is more disruptive — everybody faces uncertainty annually — and it is considerably simpler, and for a small organisation it is probably the better answer. Worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to the more complex option.
